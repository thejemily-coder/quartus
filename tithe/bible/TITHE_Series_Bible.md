# TITHE
## Series Bible — Writers' Room Edition (contains all secrets)

A prestige-drama text RPG in seasons. Grim low fantasy that becomes high, then cosmic, then strange. Mud, steel, debt and faith in Season One; gods, stars and the shape of the world by the end.

**Logline.** A broken sellsword who died for a night at a massacre and came back wrong is hired to guard a wagon into a frontier town where people are being emptied of their souls. The man who sold his company to the slaughter is the town's marshal. The stars have stopped seeing him. They want to know why.

**Show voice.** Literate, unsentimental, funny in the dark. Characters talk like people under pressure, not like fantasy novels. Violence is fast, ugly and consequential: wounds stay, bodies stay, grief stays. Sex is frank, adult and character-revealing; it is never a reward screen, it always changes something between the people involved. Every major character believes they are the hero, and most of them have a point. Nobody is safe, including people the player loves.

**Content standard.** Mature and unflinching. Graphic violence (dismemberment, torture's aftermath, plague, monstrous body horror) is depicted directly and specifically. Sexual content is explicit in an HBO-drama register: bodies, desire, awkwardness, the physical truth of it, written with heat and specificity and with a cut or ellipsis before it becomes a catalogue. All romantic and sexual characters are adults. No sexual violence is ever depicted on screen or eroticized; where it exists in backstory it is treated as the crime it is.

## 1. THE THREE ARCS

### Arc One — The Rise
Ansel Dray goes from drunk hired blade (S1E1) to Captain of Harrowgate's sworn company (S1 finale) to knight and lord-commander (S2) to warlord of the North (S3) to the man the whole world's war turns on (S4) to something that is no longer quite a man (S5). Every step up costs him a person or a principle. The player shapes *how* he rises (mercy or terror, honesty or cunning) but not *that* he rises.

### Arc Two — The Slow Burn
The game offers many romantic possibilities: Lady Isolde Varane (high-born, above him), Lampwarden Brannagh Vey (the enemy hunting him), Oriel (the oracle tied to the cosmic mystery), Tamsin Vell (the companion who rides with him from the first scene), plus trysts (Mags Halloran, Rusk the bandit queen, Delphine the courtesan-spy).

**SECRET: The true slow burn is Tamsin Vell.** She is hidden in plain sight: the flashier options get the dramatic set-pieces, while Tamsin gets the small moments (shared watches, stolen apples, bad singing, arguments). The game never labels her as the romance. Season One builds the bond, the near-kiss (E6), and the betrayal (E7) and the choice to undo it (E8). They do **not** consummate in Season One no matter what the player does; if the player pushes, Tamsin herself stops it, for reasons that become devastating in E7. Their first night together is S2E5. They break apart in S3 and come back together at the end of S4. In S5 she is the last person he sees as a man.

The other options are real, not decoys: Isolde and Ansel can share a charged, unconsummated longing in S1 (a dance, a near-kiss, a letter) and an affair in S2 after her marriage to Prince Cassius. Brannagh is enemies-to-something in S1 and a lover in S2/S3 if the player pursues it. Oriel's connection is spiritual, eerie and intimate; it becomes something else in S4.

### Arc Three — The Cosmic Plot (SECRET MYTHOLOGY)

**The Compact.** A thousand years ago the world (the old word is *the Hearth*) had its own gods: the **Deep Mothers**, vast slow intelligences living in the rock and water under the world. When people died, they were buried, and their souls went *down*, into the Mothers, into the dark warmth of the world. That was death, and it was not frightening.

Then something came from beyond the sky: **the Choir**. The stars are not stars. They are vast, beautiful, hungry beings that feed on *the light of a lived life*, which is to say on souls. The Choir offered the barrow-builder kings (the **First Kings**) a bargain: crowns, starfire, dynasties that would not fall, and protection from the Mothers' rages, in exchange for the dead. The First Kings swore the **Compact** on a standing stone at a river crossing called **Corran's Ford**. Then they built the barrows across the land as **locks**: each barrow-king lies in the earth as a living seal, keeping the Deep Mothers chained and dreaming.

The Choir founded a church to manage the harvest: **the Lamp**. The Lamp teaches that the dead rise to the stars to sing with the Saints in eternal light. That is literally true and it is a horror: the dead are eaten. Lamp funerals are pyres (the **Kindling**) so the smoke carries the soul up. Earth-burial is the oldest heresy in Aldermere, punishable by burning, because a buried soul goes down to the Mothers, out of the Choir's reach.

**The Reckoning.** The Compact has a clause. Every thousand years the Choir takes a **Great Tithe**: not just the dead, but a measure of the living. A whole land emptied. The thousandth year is close. The upper Lamp hierarchy knows. Some of its people (Abbess Morwenna Sallow) believe they can "pay down" the Reckoning by harvesting souls early, a few at a time, from the poor and forgotten. That is **the Hollowing**: people found alive, breathing, eating, but empty. No memory, no speech, no self. Their souls were drawn up through a rite called the **Small Tithe**.

**The Tallyman.** The Choir's accountant on earth: a tall grey man in a rain-dark coat who appears wherever a death is close, writing in a ledger. He counts. Every soul is in his book. He can be anywhere. He is polite. He is not a man.

**Ansel Dray is Unreckoned.** At the Siege of Corran's Ford six years ago, the Red Company was betrayed and massacred on the riverbank, around the old Compact-stone. Ansel died there, his blood soaking the stone. He lay dead through a night under the stars. And because a man died *on the Compact itself*, something slipped: his soul went neither up nor down. In the morning he woke. He carries a burn on his left palm in the shape of the seven-pointed Lamp star (the stone's sigil). Since then:
- The Tallyman cannot count him. The ledger has no line for him. He is the first soul outside the Compact in a thousand years: **a crack in the Compact**.
- The Choir's things (starfire, Saints, the Hollowing) slide off him or misjudge him.
- The Deep Mothers can feel him through the earth. To them he is a door.
- He sleeps badly under open sky. He feels watched by the stars. He is right.

**Who wants him.** *Mother Gall* (the fen-witch, servant of the Mothers) wants to bury him alive in the sacred bog so the Mothers can use him as a key and break the Compact; that would free the Mothers, and the Mothers are not kind either. *The Lamp*, through Lampwarden Brannagh and later the Hierarch, hunts "the Starless One" of prophecy as the herald of the end of the world. *The Choir* wants him erased, and in S2 starts to want him *used*: an uncountable soul could be a weapon, or food that never runs out. *Prince Cassius* wants him because he is useful. *Ansel* just wants to stop being afraid of the sky.

**Endgame (S5).** Ansel learns that he can do what the First Kings did: make a new Compact. He refuses to give the world to either the Choir or the Mothers. With Tamsin, Oriel and the remnant of the cast, he walks into the Rim (the edge of the world, where the sky meets the deep) and *breaks* the Compact, starving the Choir and loosing the Mothers, then lays himself down as the one lock both must pass through: the dead go nowhere they are eaten. What happens to souls afterward is the last mystery, and the series leaves it open. Tamsin buries him in earth, which by then is not heresy.

**How the mystery unfolds (reveal ladder).**
- S1: Hollowing is real and man-made (the Abbess's rite). Ansel is "not counted." Earth-burial heresy matters. Barrow carvings show kings kneeling to stars. A star goes out at the S1 finale.
- S2: The Lamp's inner doctrine of the Reckoning. The Tallyman speaks at length. The First Kings' Compact text is found. Oriel says what the stars are.
- S3: The Deep Mothers wake as barrows are broken. Monsters of the deep rise. The Mothers are as terrible as the Choir.
- S4: The Choir descends: Saints walk. The Great Tithe begins. The sky war.
- S5: The Rim. The breaking.

| Season | Title | Scope | Tone |

| One | The Marches | One frontier town and its hinterland | Grim, intimate, muddy. Monsters are rare and awful. Politics are petty and lethal. |

## 3. PRINCIPAL CAST

Color codes are used in the game UI.

### ANSEL DRAY — the Sellsword (protagonist; fixed, authored)
31. Born in Lowmarch, a tanner's son (he still can't stand the smell of lime). Ran off at fifteen to the Red Company, a free company of four hundred spears. Sergeant at twenty-three. At twenty-five, the Siege of Corran's Ford: Captain Konrad Hask took gold from the rebel Duke of Ashwick and marched the Red Company into a crossfire on the riverbank. Ansel died on the Compact-stone and woke in the morning among the bodies, with the crows already working.

Six years since: hired sword, caravan guard, pit-fighter for a winter, debt collector for a spring. Drinks to sleep. Hands shake in the morning. Won't sleep in the open. Still keeps the company roll, four hundred names, in a waxed leather case; he reads them some nights.

*Look:* Big through the shoulders, gone lean. A broken nose set badly. A rope of scar from left collarbone to ear. Grey coming in at the temples early. Dark eyes. The star-shaped burn on his left palm, which he keeps gloved.

*Voice:* Laconic. Says the true thing too bluntly or not at all. Gallows humor. Kind to animals and children despite himself, embarrassed when caught. Polite to servants, contemptuous of lords. When he's frightened he gets quieter.

*Want:* To kill Konrad Hask. *Need:* To be responsible for people again, and to let them be responsible for him. *Wound:* He believes he should have died with the Company and that everything since is borrowed. *Secret:* He doesn't know what he is.

*Gear:* "Widow," a battered bastard sword taken from a dead knight at Corran's Ford. A horse called Ox, an ugly, bad-tempered dun gelding who loves him.

### TAMSIN VELL — the Thief (companion from E1; the true slow burn, secret)
24. Fen-born. Archer, picklock, liar, survivor. Freckled, sunburnt, a chipped front tooth, copper hair hacked short with a knife. Laughs at funerals. Can't read and is ashamed of it. Sings badly and loudly. Steals compulsively, mostly small things she then gives away. Calls Ansel "Sergeant" to annoy him, then because it's his name to her.

*Secret:* Raised by Mother Gall in the Gallowmere fen after the Lamp burned her mother for earth-burying Tamsin's stillborn brother. Gall felt the Unreckoned man through the earth the night he died. When he came within reach, Gall sent Tamsin to find him and bring him to the fen. Tamsin's "chance" meeting in E1 is not chance. She reports to Gall by crow and by charm. She believes, at first, that the Mothers are good and the Lamp is murder; she isn't wrong about the Lamp. She falls in love with Ansel slowly and against her will, and in E7 she delivers him to Gall anyway, because she gave her word and because Gall raised her. In E8 she digs him out of the bog with her hands.

*Why she stops the consummation in S1:* she will not sleep with the man she is going to betray.

*Combat:* longbow, quick knife. Pins targets, sneak shots.

### LADY ISOLDE VARANE — the Heir (romance option; S1 political lead)
23. Only surviving child of Lord Aurel Varane of Harrowgate. Tall, dark-haired, grey-eyed, a reader with a ruinous memory for numbers. Raised to be married well; educated herself to rule anyway. Courteous like a blade is sharp. She keeps her father's books and knows Harrowgate is bankrupt and that the marriage to Prince Cassius is the only thing keeping the Crown from absorbing the March. She plays a long game and expects to lose at it.

*Want:* to save Harrowgate. *Need:* to want something for herself. *Arc S1:* from her father's clever daughter to Lady of Harrowgate after his murder (E7), ruling in her own name by E8. She hires Ansel off the books in E2 to find her missing maid; she trusts him because he has nothing to lose and nobody to sell her to. She is drawn to him because he is the only man who has ever told her a true thing in a polite room. A dance (E5), a near-kiss in the archive (E5), a letter (E8). She marries Cassius in S2.

### LAMPWARDEN BRANNAGH VEY — the Hunter (romance option; enemy)
27. A warrior-inquisitor of the Lamp. Close-cropped white-blonde hair, a face like a church carving, a burn scar down her throat from the starfire she channels. Peasant-born, given to the Lamp at eight, the best blade in her chapter. She mortifies herself with a knotted cord at night. She believes completely, and she is not stupid, which is her tragedy.

Comes to Harrowgate in E3 with a company of Lampwardens and a caged oracle, sent to find "a soul that is not counted," which the Lamp assumes is a witch or a demon-host. She burns a "witch" in E3 (the player can save the girl). She and Ansel are drawn to each other through violence and argument: they're the two best killers in the March and they know it. In E5 they spar. In E7 she interrogates him. In E8 she discovers the Abbess's Hollowing rite in the Lanternhold crypt and turns on her own church to stop it. By S2 she is a heretic on the run.

*Combat (when allied):* sword and starfire; starfire burns undead and Under things; it *does nothing to Ansel* (a key E7 reveal).

### ORIEL — the Oracle (romance option; cosmic key)
21. The Lamp's "Saint-touched" oracle, kept in an iron-and-glass carriage-cage that travels with Brannagh's company. Born blind; sees by starlight, literally: she hears the Choir. Silver-white eyes, shaven head tattooed with star charts, thin as a wire. Often speaks in a voice that isn't hers. When it's her own voice she is dry, curious, starved for touch, very young in some ways and ancient in others.

She can't see Ansel. To her he's a hole in the singing, and in that silence she feels, for the first time in her life, *alone in her own head*. It is the most intimate thing that has ever happened to her. "You are not counted," she tells him in E3. Freed in E8 (player choice of how). In S2 she becomes the voice of the mystery.

### SER KONRAD HASK — the Marshal (S1 antagonist)
46. Former captain of the Red Company. Sold it at Corran's Ford for the Duke of Ashwick's gold and a pardon, then sold the Duke to the Crown. Now Marshal of Harrowgate: commander of Lord Varane's men-at-arms. Handsome in a ruined way, warm, funny, generous to his men, a patient listener. Believes in nothing but himself and the next deal. He is the Abbess's partner in the Hollowing: his men round up the vagrants, debtors and fen-folk she needs, and he sells the "emptied" ones as labor to the Saltdown mines (a Hollowed man works without food complaints until he dies). He is also Prince Cassius's man, positioning to take Harrowgate when Varane falls.

He recognizes Ansel at the gate in E1 and, instead of killing him, offers him work. He likes Ansel. He really did. He'll explain why he did what he did, and it'll almost make sense. Ansel kills him in E8, or spares him into a fate worse than death (player choice).

### ABBESS MORWENNA SALLOW — the Abbess (S1 hidden antagonist)
62. Head of the Lanternhold, the Lamp's chapterhouse in Harrowgate. Plump, motherly, smells of honey and lamp oil, runs the town's only hospital and orphanage. Beloved. Performs the Small Tithe in the crypt beneath the Lanternhold. She believes the Reckoning is coming and that every soul she harvests early spares a hundred later. She's doing arithmetic with people and she's very good at it. She has never once enjoyed it.

### LORD AUREL VARANE — the Lord (S1, dies E7)
55. Lord of Harrowgate. Gouty, kind, weak, beloved by his daughter, mocked by his marshal. Gambled the March's money on a canal that was never finished. Knows he has failed. Murdered by Hask in E7.

### PRINCE CASSIUS ALDERMERE — the Prince (S1 guest, S2 lead antagonist/ally)
30. Heir to the dying King Osric III. Golden, charming, amused, intelligent, cruel without heat. Betrothed to Isolde. Arrives E5 for the Feast of Lanterns. Backs Hask's coup quietly, and abandons Hask the moment it fails. Takes an interest in Ansel ("I collect things that shouldn't exist").

### MOTHER GALL — the Fen-Witch (S1, in between)
Older than anyone can say. Tiny, toothless, bright-eyed, hairy-chinned, delightful, terrifying. Lives on a stilt-house in the Gallowmere fen surrounded by bog-sunk bodies she calls her "sleepers." Midwife, abortionist, healer, curse-maker. She and her kind have kept the old earth-burial in secret for a thousand years. She raised Tamsin and loves her in her way. Sees the Choir as murderers and the Lamp as their butchers. Correct. Plans to bury Ansel alive as a key for the Mothers. Mentor, manipulator, villain, victim. In E8 she can be killed, spared, or left to the Lampwardens.

### THE TALLYMAN (recurring, cosmic)
A tall grey man in a rain-dark greatcoat, wide-brimmed hat, clean pale hands, a ledger bound in something like skin. Polite, mild, faintly bored. Appears at the edges of scenes where death is coming: at the back of a tavern, at the edge of a battle, beside a sickbed. Only the dying, the oracle and Ansel can see him clearly. He cannot find Ansel in the book and it bothers him the way a wrong sum bothers a clerk. In E8's final scene, he writes a new line, in the capital, under a new heading.

### Companions joining in Season One

Knows the Lamp's scripture backwards, including the heresies. Comic, then tragic. Joins E2. *Combat:* a cudgel, a battered prayer-book; once per fight can Litany (heal/steady). Ansel can train him (bond). *E7 fate:* Hob dies in the coup **unless** Ansel trained him (flag `e2_hob_hired` and bond.hob ≥ 3, *and* the player sends Hob to warn Isolde rather than to the gate in E7). His death or survival echoes forever.
- **ULLA STONEHAND**, 38. Nordvik shieldmaiden, six and a half feet, braided red-grey hair, missing two fingers, gay, warm, blunt, enormous appetite for food, songs and fights. Exiled for killing her chieftain's son, who raped her sister; she doesn't regret it. Working as a mine guard at Saltdown when Ansel meets her; joins E4. *Combat:* axe and shield; taunts enemies onto herself.

## 4. THE WORLD (Season One footprint)

**Aldermere.** A kingdom of fields, forests and stone towns ruled from Corvane by the dying King Osric III. Feudal, poor, pious. Steel is expensive; silver more so. Most people never meet a monster. When they do, they mostly die.

**The March of Harrowgate.** A border province on the wet western edge of the kingdom.
- **Harrowgate** — a walled hill-town of four thousand: the castle (Varane Keep), the **Lanternhold** (chapterhouse, hospital, orphanage; crypts beneath), the Market Stair, the Tanners' Bottom, the **Gutted Hen** tavern, the cisterns under the old town.
- **The Kingsroad** — muddy, bandit-haunted road east toward Corvane. Waystations, gibbets, the burned village of **Ashby** (E1).
- **Gallowmere Fen** — the great bog to the south. Stilt-villages, eel-weirs, will-o'-wisps the locals call **Lantern Men**, drowned dead, and Mother Gall's house.
- **Saltdown** — salt and silver mines in the chalk hills north, worked by debtors and, lately, the Hollowed.
- **The Thornwood** — dense forest west; bandits, boar, the Sorrowhound, the old troll bridge.
- **The Barrowfields** — the downs east of the fen, crowned with grassy barrows; the greatest is the **Barrow of the Nine Crowns**.
- **Corran's Ford** — two days east; the river crossing and the Compact-stone. Seen only in flashback in S1.

**The Lamp.** Clergy: Lamplighters (priests), Lampwardens (warrior-inquisitors, wear white-enamelled plate and a seven-point star), the Hierarch in Corvane. Rites: the Kindling (pyre-funeral), the Evening Lamp (prayer at dusk), the Tithe (church tax, ten percent: "tithe-silver"). Oaths: "By the Lamp," "Saints keep you," "Light take it." Curse: "Earthbound" (one who'll be buried) is the worst insult.

**Old ways.** Hedge-witches, earth-burial in secret, the bog-sacrifices, barrow-offerings. Oaths: "Mothers below," "Go down easy." The fen-folk leave a bowl of milk by the water for the dead.

**Magic in S1.** Rare and costly. Hedge charms (crow-messages, luck knots, sleep-smoke), Lamp starfire (Lampwardens can make their blades burn with cold blue light; it hurts them), barrow-runes. Monsters are the most common magic.

**Money.** Silver pennies ("silver"). A night at the Hen: 5. A poultice: 12. A good sword: 150. A man's life in the Saltdown mines: 40.

## 6. GAME SYSTEMS (for writers)

- **Stats** (0–6+): Might (force, intimidation), Finesse (speed, stealth, aim, picking locks), Grit (endurance, willpower, resisting fear and drink), Wits (perception, knowledge, tactics), Presence (persuasion, command, seduction, lies). Ansel starts Might 3, Finesse 2, Grit 3, Wits 2, Presence 1.
- **Checks:** d20 + stat×2 (+ bonuses) vs DC. Easy 10, Moderate 13, Hard 16, Very Hard 19, Heroic 22.
- **Bonds** (−3…+10) with Tamsin, Isolde, Brannagh, Oriel, Hob, Pell, Ulla, Mags. **Reputation** with Town (Harrowgate folk), Varane (the house), Lamp (the church), Fen (old ways).
- **Unreckoned** skill branch unlocks in E4 (flag `unreckoned`), when the Tallyman fails to count Ansel in front of witnesses.
- **Wounds** linger until treated. **Death** = retry the fight.
- **Between episodes:** Harrowgate hub (inn, market, smith, apothecary/crafting, notice board contracts, the Wilds for random encounters/foraging, companion talks).

## 7. SEASON ONE — "THE MARCHES" (8 episodes)

**Season engine.** A-plot: who is Hollowing the poor of Harrowgate, and Hask's coup. Rise: Ansel from hired sword to Captain of the Harrowgate company. Romance: Tamsin's bond grows; Isolde's longing; Brannagh's collision; Oriel's silence. Cosmic: the Tallyman can't count him; the barrow carvings; a star goes out.

**Season end-state (canon for S2):** Lord Varane dead; Lady Isolde rules Harrowgate; Hask dead or worse; the Abbess dead or arrested; Ansel Captain of Harrowgate's sworn company "the Dead Men"; Brannagh a deserter from the Lamp; Oriel free and travelling with Ansel; Tamsin forgiven or not, but present; Prince Cassius withdrawn to Corvane, interested. Over Harrowgate one star is missing. The Tallyman opens a new page.

### E1 — "The Dead Don't Pay"
*Cold open:* Corran's Ford, six years ago. Ansel, 25, on the riverbank as the crossfire hits; Hask on the far bank, turning his horse away. Ansel dies on the stone. Night. Stars. A grey man with a ledger walks among the bodies counting... and stops at Ansel, and frowns, and turns the page back, and forward. Dawn: Ansel's eyes open. Crows.
*A-plot:* The Crooked Mile waystation, autumn. Ansel, hungover, broke, is hired by cloth merchant Odo Pettibone to guard his wagon to Harrowgate. Tamsin is in the yard, "also hired" (she bribed her way on). On the Kingsroad: a deserter ambush (spare or kill the boy Wat → `e1_spared_wat`); the burned village of Ashby, where the villagers are standing in the rain, Hollowed (mercy-kill, leave, or try to lead them → `e1_ashby`); night camp: ghouls come for the dead and then for the living. Odo is dragged off and eaten. Ghoul Brute fight. Tamsin and Ansel arrive at Harrowgate at dawn with the wagon.

### E2 — "Harrowgate"

*A-plot:* Ansel looks for work. Lady Isolde summons him through her old nurse: her maid Annet vanished from the Tanners' Bottom; Hask's watch won't look. She pays well and wants it off the books. *B-plot (monster of the week):* The cisterns: rat swarms, a drowned smuggler's cache, and **the Wedded Rats**, a rat-king the size of a cart. Method: fire (flood the tunnel with lamp oil; burns the smugglers' goods too) or fight (→ `e2_ratking`='burned'|'fought').

*Rise:* Town gossip: "the man who killed the rat-king." Rep Town up. Pell joins (`e2_pell_joined`).

*End:* On the Lanternhold steps at dusk, the Abbess blesses the poor. Hask beside her. They exchange a look.
*Next time:* The Lamp comes to Harrowgate.

### E3 — "The Lamp Comes"

### E4 — "Salt"
*Cold open:* Saltdown, the deep galleries. Hollowed miners work without lamps, silently. A great blind head comes out of the salt and eats one. The others keep working.
*A-plot:* Lord Varane hires Ansel and his people to find why Saltdown's output has collapsed (miners vanishing). Hask is furious; it's his concession. At Saltdown, Ulla Stonehand, mine guard, a bear of a woman with no illusions; she joins (`e4_ulla`). Discovery: most miners are Hollowed, brought from Harrowgate in Hask's carts; the mine overseer keeps a ledger of "deliveries from the Lanternhold" (`e4_ledger` if taken; Finesse/Wits). *Cosmic (mid-season turn):* In the deep, a miner dying of a wyrm bite. The Tallyman is there, counting. Everyone present who is dying, and Ansel, see him. He looks at Ansel, opens his ledger, and turns pages, and says, quietly, puzzled: "You're not here." He's gone. Ansel's palm burns. **Unlock the Unreckoned branch** (`unreckoned`=1). ### E6 — "The Barrow"
*Cold open:* A thousand years ago, a king in bronze lies down alive in his tomb while his priests seal the door; above, the stars sing.
*Bottle episode:* A contract (from Pell's research, or Varane) to the Barrow of the Nine Crowns, where grave-robbers are dying. Ansel and Tamsin (plus one companion, player's choice) are sealed in by a storm and a collapse. Wights. The carvings: kings kneeling before stars, the stars with mouths. Pell (if present) reads them. Barrow-King Hollin, a living lock, mad: he can be fought or talked to (Wits/Presence). He tells Ansel "you are a door" and begs to be relieved. Ansel can take his ring (`e6_ring`, trinket "Barrow-King's Ring") — taking it weakens the lock (consequence S3).
*Romance:* Tamsin and Ansel in the dark, waiting for air to run out. Her past (Lamp burned her mother); he tells her about the night at the Ford. The near-kiss (`e6_tam_kiss`='almost'). If the player pushes for more, Tamsin stops it: "Not like this. Not with what I — not like this." (`e6_tam_stopped`). Bond up regardless of path.
*End:* They dig out at dawn. The downs are full of crows. Tamsin is quiet the whole ride home.

### E7 — "Wolves at the Gate"
*Cold open:* Hask in Varane's solar at night, pouring the old lord a drink. Talking about his own father. Then he stabs Varane through the throat with a letter knife and holds him while he dies. Then he calls for the guard, weeping.
*A-plot:* Hask's coup: Lord Varane "murdered by the dead sergeant." Hask's men and Brannagh's Lampwardens hunt Ansel through Harrowgate at night. Hob's fate (`e7_hob`='alive'|'dead'). Wat at the postern if spared in E1. Mags hides people. Isolde under guard in the Keep. Ansel captured by Brannagh at the Lanternhold (fixed).
*Interrogation:* Brannagh's cell. She tries starfire on him to make him confess: it does nothing. Her faith cracks visibly. Oriel brought to him; she tells Brannagh, in the Choir's voice, to burn him; then in her own, "they can't see him." (`e7_brannagh_doubt`). Possible charged moment with Brannagh through bars (player choice; restrained).
*The betrayal:* Tamsin breaks him out (a slick, joyful rescue), leads him through the fen to "safety"... to Mother Gall. Drugged wine. The reveal: Tamsin is Gall's. Gall's sleepers drag him into the bog. Tamsin watches. Ansel's last line to her is a player choice (curse, forgive, silence → `e7_last_words`). The mud closes over his face.
*End:* Black. Underground. Something vast, warm, enormous, turning over in its sleep. A voice made of stone: *Door.*

### E8 — "The Tithe"
*Cold open:* In the bog, in the dark, Ansel dreams the Deep Mothers' dream: the world before the stars, then he's back at Corran's Ford counting the four hundred. Then hands, digging: Tamsin, bloody-fingered, crying, hauling him out of the bog. Gall screaming.
*A-plot:* Gall's reckoning (kill, spare, or leave her to the drowned she wakes; `e8_gall`). The ride back: Brannagh, deserting, finds them; she has found the Abbess's crypt, the Small Tithe ledgers, the Hollowed children (`e8_brannagh`='ally'). Assemble the company: Ulla, Pell, Hob (if alive), Mags's people, Moll (if turned), freed Oriel. Plan: tonight the Abbess performs the largest Small Tithe yet, on the Lanternhold's orphans, to pay for Hask's elevation. Retake the Keep (free Isolde) and stop the rite.
*Climax:* Storm the Lanternhold. Pell at the crypt door (`e8_pell`='alive'|'dead'). Duel with Hask on the crypt stair: "We were the same, Dray." (kill / spare into the Saltdown mines as a Hollowed-man's laborer / hand him to Isolde's justice → `e8_hask`). The rite: a **Lantern Saint** descends, a column of singing light full of faces. Starfire can't touch it; Ansel can. He kills it. Outside, over Harrowgate, a star goes out.
*Resolution:* The Abbess (dead by her own Saint, or arrested by Brannagh, `e8_abbess`). Cassius withdraws ("Charming town. I'll be back for the wedding."). Isolde names Ansel Captain of Harrowgate's sworn company; he names it "the Dead Men." Oriel, freed: "They've seen you now." Tamsin and Ansel on the wall-walk at dawn: no forgiveness yet, no absence either.
*Final shot:* Corvane, the capital. The Tallyman in a candlelit archive under the Hierarch's palace. He turns to a fresh page and writes a heading: **ANSEL DRAY.** Below it, nothing. Then, slowly, he writes: *Owed.*

## 8. SEASONS TWO – FIVE (forward plan)

**S2 — The Crown.** Isolde's wedding to Cassius in Corvane; King Osric's death; succession war between Cassius and his half-sister Queen-in-exile Maud. Ansel knighted (S2E3) and made lord-commander. The Hierarch of the Lamp; the inner doctrine of the Reckoning; Brannagh's heresy trial; Oriel's prophecies to the court. Tamsin and Ansel together (S2E5). The Tallyman's long conversation (S2E6). A second star falls, visibly, onto Corvane: something walks out of the crater. Finale: Cassius crowned by a bargain with the Lamp; Ansel exiled north; Isolde pregnant (whose?).

**S3 — The Barrows.** The North; Ulla's people; the barrows breaking open as the locks fail (Ansel's ring from E6 among the causes). The Deep Mothers' children rise: burrowing horrors, the drowned armies, giants of root and bone. Ansel becomes warlord of the North. Gall's sisters. Tamsin leaves him. Hob (if alive) dies or becomes a legend. Brannagh as a lover and a crusader. Finale: a Mother wakes; a mountain opens its eye.

**S4 — The Choir.** The Great Tithe begins. Saints descend: cities silenced. Cassius's crown was the price. Isolde's betrayal and redemption. Oriel becomes the Choir's voice and then its traitor. The sky war. Tamsin returns. Finale: Ansel learns he can make a Compact.

**S5 — The Unreckoned.** The Rim. The inside of the world. The breaking. The burial.
