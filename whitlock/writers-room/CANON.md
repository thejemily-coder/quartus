# Whitlock — Series canon (shared with the player)

SERIES: WHITLOCK — an interactive prestige-TV college drama.
SETTING: Whitlock University (est. 1791), an old-money private university in Alder Falls, a small snowy town in the Berkshires of western Massachusetts. Gothic towers, ivy, legacies vs. scholarship kids. Present day: phones, group chats, Instagram, TikTok, read receipts, screenshots as weapons.
KEY PLACES: Lowell House (oldest dorm; Cal has a coveted single on the 3rd floor — his father pulled strings); Elm Row (fraternity row; Sigma Kappa Rho "the Rho" is the oldest house, a mansion whose alumni include senators and felons); Kappa Lambda sorority house; Whitaker Commons (student center built with Cal's grandfather's money — his name is literally on the building); The Whitlock Herald (student paper, basement of Pruitt Hall); Kessler Rink (varsity hockey); the Tamsin Lake boathouse (rowing); Marlowe Theater (drama dept.); Fennimore Library; The Anchor (townie bar on Mill Street owned by Gus Moretti); Dot's Diner; the town of Alder Falls itself, where locals resent the school.
WORLDS: Greek life is central (Rho rush/pledging: hazing, loyalty, brotherhood, rot); also the Herald, varsity athletics, theater/arts, and the townies.
PROTAGONIST: CAL WHITAKER, 18, freshman. Third-generation legacy and black sheep: he got in on the name and everyone knows it. Charming screwup — funny, magnetic, avoidant, self-sabotaging; easy to love, hard to rely on. Smart, doesn't try (a professor will see through him; academic probation is possible). Secret gifts: he is a genuinely gifted writer (notebooks no one has read) and reads people uncannily — he knows what they want to hear, a superpower and a curse. Vices: lies easily (small smoothing lies that stack); runs when it's real (bolts when someone gets close or things get serious). Central wound: his mother Claire walked out when he was eleven; his cold, powerful father Richard raised him (mostly via housekeepers and St. Anselm's boarding school in New Hampshire) and expects him to be a credit. The school is Dad's turf. Cal is straight.
FATHER: RICHARD WHITAKER, 51, Whitlock '93, Rho alum, trustee, Boston finance. Magnetic, emotionally unavailable; calls rarely and always wants something. Expects Cal to pledge the Rho.
MOTHER: CLAIRE WHITAKER, 49, a writer, left when Cal was 11. Whereabouts unknown to Cal. She can surface offscreen (a text, a voicemail, a sighting) when the show decides.
PILOT: Move-in day. Cal arrives alone in a car service; Dad was supposed to come and didn't. By sunset he's at a party.
TONE: an even split of comedy and drama — real laughs and real damage every episode. Influences: Euphoria (raw, intimate, stylized darkness), Gilmore Girls (rapid literate banter, small-town warmth), Greek (Greek-life politics), How I Met Your Mother and Friends (ensemble chemistry, running bits, friendship as family), Entourage (the boys, status, excess). Mix sweetness, sadness, warmth, cruelty, despair, joy, darkness and hope.
STRUCTURE: 10 episodes per season; each season covers about a month (summer seasons cover the whole summer, faster). Fall and spring semesters each hold three seasons. Four years at Whitlock. Ensemble cast that grows gradually; across years students arrive, graduate, go abroad and come back, fail out.

## Writers' room standards

- Voice: third person, present tense, written like a prestige-TV script translated into lean, vivid prose: scene headings, action, sharp dialogue. Cal is the protagonist; the camera follows him, with occasional short cutaways to scenes he is NOT in (the audience sees them; Cal does not).
- Tone: an even split of comedy and drama. Banter should crackle (Gilmore Girls speed, HIMYM/Friends ensemble rhythm, Entourage swagger, Euphoria intimacy and darkness). Every episode mixes at least three of: sweetness, sadness, warmth, cruelty, despair, joy, darkness, hope.
- Content: the player is an adult who opted into fully mature content. Be explicit, frank and unflinching about sex, drugs, drinking, violence, abuse, grief, mental illness, self-harm, racism, class cruelty and other hard subjects when the story calls for it. Sex scenes have real physicality, specificity and emotional texture; violence has weight and aftermath. Hard material always serves character and story and carries consequences — never linger for shock or exploitation. Everyone in any sexual or romantic content is 18 or older; minors (e.g. Jess's brother Danny) are never sexualized.
- Stakes: Cal can be hurt, humiliated, injured, arrested, put on academic or disciplinary probation, lose friends and lovers — "bruised, not broken": he never dies and never permanently leaves Whitlock.
- Characters are specific people with their own wants and lives — not props for Cal. Nobody is purely good or bad.
- KNOWLEDGE DISCIPLINE (critical): a character may only say, reference or act on information that (a) the FACTS list shows they know (their id in knownBy), (b) they witnessed or were told on-screen in this episode, or (c) is public. Cal never knows what happened in cutaways. If someone learns something new, show or state how they learned it. Characters who have not met Cal (metCal false) treat him as a stranger (though they may know of the Whitaker name).
- Continuity: respect every established fact, relationship, date, location and earlier choice. Never contradict the canon ledger.
