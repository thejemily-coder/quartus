# THE EVENT HORIZON: Series Bible (SEALED)

*A choice-driven superhero drama in the shape of prestige television. Philadelphia, then everywhere, then everything.*

Mature audiences only. Every character in any sexual content is an adult (both leads are 18 at the pilot). Content is uncut: there is no fade-to-black setting.

---

## 1. The Pitch

Sam Kearney is an 18-year-old from South Philly, the first in his family to go to college. He moves into a Temple University dorm on a Thursday. On Saturday night every clock in North Philadelphia stops for seven-tenths of a second, and Sam keeps moving.

Juliet Pemberton is the Main Line surgeon's daughter on his floor. She thinks he's a loud, sweaty child, and he thinks she's a snob. On the same night she was changed too. Neither of them knows the other was.

*The Event Horizon* is about the long, uneven process of Sam becoming someone who deserves the word *hero*, and about the woman who becomes his partner in every sense along the way. It starts as a charming street-level comedy about a pizza-delivery kid with super-speed and grows, season by season, into something vast, strange, and devastating. The neighborhood grows into the city, then the country, the hemisphere, the world. Then it reaches time, other dimensions, and space, and finally the outer edge of the universe itself.

**The central dramatic question:** Sam's flaw is that he *has to be the one* who saves everyone, and his power is time. Every season makes it easier for him to go back and fix it. Somewhere out there is a version of him who never stopped going back. The show asks whether our Sam will learn to let other people carry weight and to let some things stay broken, or whether he will become that other man.

## 2. Tone Rules

1. **Sweet, silly, emotional, powerful.** All four, in every season, in changing proportions. Season 1 is about 70% sweet and silly. The final seasons are about 70% emotional and powerful, but the jokes never stop entirely, because these people use jokes to survive.
2. **Darkness creeps in at the edges.** Grief, horror, and violence arrive slowly. Never front-load them. One shadow per episode in Season 1, more as the seasons advance. When horror arrives it is *specific*: a smell, a sound, a wrongness in a familiar place.
3. **Character-driven, raw, mature, explicit.** Sex, violence, and grief are written uncut and on the page, but always through character. The first time Sam and Jules sleep together is written as two specific people, nervous and funny and overwhelmed, not as generic choreography. A broken arm is written as a South Philly kid trying not to cry in front of his brother. Gore exists to make violence cost something, never to make it cool.
4. **The romance is earned.** Season 1: they can't stand each other for about three episodes, then fall hard as ordinary people. Then a long, layered slow burn. Season 1 is her boyfriend plus both their secrets. Season 2 is the mutual reveal and then bad timing. The target is for them to get together by the end of Season 3, through near-misses, other relationships, and a drunken almost. What finally makes it irresistible is trauma-bonding: saving the city together, night after night. Player choices can delay it, break it, or hurt it. Write to the save.
5. **Nobody is a villain in their own head.** Teddy Kowal robs armored cars to pay for his mother's dialysis. Whitney Pemberton genuinely believes he is saving his family. Old Sam genuinely believes he is saving Jules.
6. **Philadelphia is a character.** Wawa, SEPTA, the El, water ice, the Italian Market, Broad Street, rowhouse stoops, the Schuylkill, the Navy Yard, the smell of the river in August, Eagles fandom as religion. Use real places freely for texture, but invent any business or building where something bad happens.
7. **Comic-book DNA, prestige-TV form.** Every episode has a cold open, a main title, act breaks, and a tag. The visual language evolves by season (see §9).

## 3. The World

### Philadelphia
- **North Philly / Temple:** Sam and Jules's dorm, **Cardone Hall** (fictional), a 14-floor 1970s tower on N. Broad near Cecil B. Moore Ave. Their floor is 9. Liacouras Walk, the Bell Tower, the rowhouses around campus, Broad Street Line stops (Cecil B. Moore, Girard, Spring Garden).
- **Ciro's Pizza** (fictional), on Cecil B. Moore Ave. Sam delivers on an e-bike and sometimes works the oven. The owner is Ciro Bianchi, 60s, a Neapolitan who speaks entirely in insults and loves Sam.
- **South Philly / East Passyunk:** the Kearney rowhouse on **Mifflin Street** near the 1700 block of E. Passyunk. Nonna Teresa's stoop is three doors down. Also the church (St. Rita's on Broad is real; use **St. Monica's** for the family parish) and the Italian Market.
- **The Main Line:** **Ravenscourt**, the Pemberton estate in Gladwyne, 1891 stone with a family chapel no one enters. The Pembertons' Rittenhouse Square penthouse, where Catherine actually lives during the week.
- **Halcyon Dynamics** (fictional): a "clean energy and materials" startup in the restored **Baldwin Locomotive Works machine shop at Broad & Spring Garden** (historically Baldwin was there, but the restoration is fictional). It also has a large campus at the **Navy Yard**. This is ground zero.

### The Event (public version)
On **Saturday, August 22, at 11:51:04 PM**, a "transformer cascade" at Halcyon's Broad Street facility caused a blackout across North Philadelphia, a visible auroral glow over the city, and "a moment of disorientation" reported by thousands. Every clock, phone, and pacemaker inside a 1.9-mile radius lost **0.7 seconds**. Phones simply skip from 11:51:04 to 11:51:05. The Inquirer calls it **"the Hitch."** Two people died (a cardiac event and a fall). Dozens were injured. Halcyon pays settlements fast.

### The Event (truth, layered, sealed)
Reveal in this order across seasons:
1. **(Seasons 1–2) It wasn't an accident.** Halcyon was running **Project APERTURE**, an attempt to open a micro-breach in spacetime to harvest "zero-point" energy. It worked for 0.7 seconds. Anyone inside the radius with *latent sensitivity* was changed. That was **61 people**, the Changed (Halcyon's internal term: "Exposed Subjects ES-01 to ES-61"). Sam is **ES-17**. Jules is **ES-44**.
2. **(Seasons 3–4) It was funded, and it was meant to happen in Philadelphia.** Whitney Pemberton's fund financed Aperture. Philadelphia sits on a **seam**, a place where the boundary of the universe is thin. There have been earlier seam events: **1876** (the Centennial Exposition in Fairmount Park, when the Corliss Engine stopped for "a heartbeat") and **1918** (the Liberty Loan parade, the flu, and "the night the bells stopped"). Halcyon picked Broad Street on purpose.
3. **(Seasons 5–8) The latent sensitivity is inherited.** Two bloodlines matter:
   - **The Pemberton line.** In 1876, the industrialist **Josiah Pemberton** stood beside the Corliss Engine when it stopped and *something on the other side noticed him*. He made a bargain he only half understood: fortune for the line, and in return, "when the door opens again, a Pemberton will hold it." The family's luck since then has been supernatural. Jules's power is the bargain finally coming due in a body. **Constance Pemberton** knows. Whitney knows part of it and wants to renegotiate the deal for more. **Catherine** married in and did not know until Season 3, and her horror is genuine.
   - **The DiNardo line** (Sam's mother's side). In 1918, **Assunta DiNardo**, Nonna Teresa's mother, then 16, stepped "between the seconds" when the bells stopped and pulled a child out from under a streetcar. The DiNardo women have carried time-sight ever since (Nonna's "feelings," Rosa's uncanny timing). Nonna always claimed Sam was the first DiNardo **man** in whom it woke. She lied (S2E2): her older brother **Salvatore DiNardo** (1931–1961) had it, never learned to stop, came back old from a fire on Arch Street, and died at the Christmas Eve Feast at 11:51. Sam is named for him. Gigi will be next (Season 7).
4. **(Seasons 9–16) It's happening everywhere.** The Horizon is thinning globally. Halcyon is one of several groups racing to exploit seams: Hanford, Houston, the Atacama, Tunguska, Lagos, Seoul, the Mariana Trench. The Philadelphia seam was the first of a new cycle.
5. **(Seasons 17–23) The Horizon is literal.** Our universe is the interior of a black hole in a larger reality. Its boundary is an **event horizon**. The seams are places where information leaks across. On the other side is **the Audience**: vast, attentive beings for whom our universe is something to watch. The Audience doesn't hate us. It *enjoys* us. It nudges events toward drama. **Jules's power is literally the Audience's favor**: probability bends toward her because she is interesting to watch. **Sam's power is the time-axis of the Horizon itself**, which the Audience can see across but not move along. The Pemberton bargain was made with a member of the Audience. The finale's question is what you do about beings who love your suffering because it's a good story.
   - **Meta-layer (never stated outright until the final seasons):** the player is the Audience. The diegetic dice are the Audience's hand. "Previously on," the recap percentages, and the title cards are all the Audience's interface. In the final season the show may address the player directly, once, devastatingly.

### Old Sam: the Revenant (sealed, show-long)
A future Sam Kearney, from a timeline where **Jules died at age 24** (in his timeline, during the Season 6 crisis). He went back to save her. It worked, and someone else died. He went back again, and again. Hundreds of loops have saturated him with **Drift**: he exists in several moments at once, and he is scarred, gray at the temples, missing the ring finger of his left hand (a loop gone wrong), and smells faintly of ozone and wet pennies.
- **Pemberton luck predates the Hitch.** The line has always been lucky (the bargain). The Hitch turned a family tendency into a power. Small impossibilities around Jules before 11:51:04 (the pool ball stopping on Friday night) are canon.
- **Pilot:** he appears once, across Broad Street in the rain, after Sam's first good deed. He puts a finger to his lips, and when a bus passes, he's gone. That's the "one shadow."
- **Season 1:** two more glimpses (Episodes 5 and 9). In Episode 9 he leaves a note in Sam's handwriting: *"Don't trust the lucky one."* He means Jules, because in his timeline her power (the Audience's favor) is what drew the Audience's attention to them both. He is wrong about what that means, but not entirely wrong.
- **Seasons 2–6:** he intervenes more, sometimes saving people and sometimes killing them, always to steer toward a future where Jules survives.
- **Seasons 7–8:** he moves into the open. The "Revenant War."
- **Endgame:** player-dependent. If our Sam has learned to let things stay broken, he can be the one who finally convinces the Revenant to stop. If he hasn't, he *becomes* the Revenant, and the show shows us how. The ledger tracks a hidden stat, **`revenant_drift`**, that rises with every "I can fix it" choice (rewinds, overriding other people's agency, refusing help) and falls with every choice to trust, accept, or share the weight.

## 5. Main Characters (summary; full voice sheets in CHARACTERS.md)

**Salvatore "Sam" Kearney** (ES-17). 18, Temple freshman, undeclared. South Philly, Italian-Irish. Works delivery at Ciro's. *Public:* funny, loud, kind, impulsive, the kid who gets in fights for other people. *Private:* he believes deep down that if he isn't useful he isn't loved. His older brother is the family hero and his sister worships him, so he has to *earn* his place. *Flaw:* hothead savior. *Powers:* the **Slip**. It looks like super-speed. It's actually perception and movement outside normal time: he steps "between the seconds." Over the seasons: Burst → Slip → Rewind → Freeze → Echo → Traverse → Anchor.

**Juliet "Jules" Pemberton** (ES-44). 18, Temple Honors, pre-med under duress. Gladwyne, old money. *Public:* cool, precise, cutting, effortlessly competent, a snob (or so Sam thinks). *Private:* she was raised to perform perfection and is terrified of being ordinary. She chose Temple over Penn as the first act of rebellion in her life, and she secretly runs a high-stakes poker game for Main Line kids. *Arc:* control → chaos → trust, plus the family inheritance. *Powers:* **probability**. Odds bend toward her without her trying. Over the seasons: Luck → Nudge → Jinx → Branch → Fate. Hidden in Season 1: when she is near Sam during a failed roll, her luck may flip a die. *She doesn't know she's doing it for him.*

## 6. Ensemble (summary)

- **Nikhil "Nik" Bhattacharya.** 18, Sam's roommate, from Edison NJ, CS major. Bengali-American, anxious, verbose, hilarious, gamer, "guy in the chair." Secretly the bravest person in the show: he is terrified of everything and does it anyway. Possible tragic arc in Seasons 13–16, possible happy one: write to the save.
- **Simone Okonkwo-Hayes.** 18, Jules's roommate, from Atlanta (Decatur), theater major. Black, queer, loud, magnetic, devastatingly perceptive. Gets the best lines. She will be the first to figure out *both* secrets (Season 1, Episode 9 target). She never gets powers, and that is her arc: the most important person in a room full of gods.
- **Vincenzo "Vinnie" Russo.** 18, Sam's best friend since kindergarten. Stayed in South Philly as an apprentice laborer (Laborers' Local 57, real-ish). Funny, proud, loyal, a little bitter that Sam left. The Iannucci family recruits him in Season 2. He can go dark, and he can come back. Possible Changed later (Season 4: a seam exposure).
- **Grace Lim.** 21, the 9th-floor RA, a pre-med senior from Cheltenham, Korean-American. Deadpan, exhausted, kind. Becomes the off-the-books medic. She stitches Sam up in Episode 4. Later, the team doctor, and perhaps Changed herself (Season 5).
- **Kearney family.** **Frank** (52, cab driver for Liberty Cab, Irish, bad back, quiet, gambling debt to the Iannucci family he's hiding). **Rosa** (49, née DiNardo, a school nurse at a South Philly elementary, sees everything, the first family member to find out; Season 2 target). **Nonna Teresa DiNardo** (81, runs the block from her stoop, knows about 1918, says strange true things). **Michael "Mikey" Kearney** (26, Army veteran with one Afghanistan deployment in 2020, now a PPD patrol officer in the 3rd District, the family hero, investigates "the Blur"). **Gianna "Gigi" Kearney** (13, eighth grade, idolizes Sam, will get powers in Season 7).
- **Pemberton family.** **Dr. Catherine Pemberton** (51, Chief of Surgery at Penn Medicine/HUP, cold, brilliant; Halcyon pays her as a "medical advisor," and she becomes complicit, then horrified). **Whitney Pemberton** (55, managing partner of Pemberton Lyle Capital, charming and absent, funds Aperture, knows about the bargain). **Hugh Pemberton** (18, Jules's twin, a Penn/Wharton freshman, golden, the favorite, competitive; not Changed, and envious of it; joins Halcyon in Season 3; ally or villain by the save). **Constance Pemberton** (84, "Gran," the matriarch of Ravenscourt, sharper than anyone, knows the bargain, and loves Jules in a way that may be ownership).
- **Callum Whitford.** 19, Jules's high school boyfriend (Episcopal Academy), now at Penn, a rower. Decent, earnest, a little boring. He loves her. That's the problem.

## 7. Antagonists (Season 1–2 detail; later ones sketched)

- **Theodore "Teddy" Kowal** (ES-09). 20, from Port Richmond (Polish), dropped out of Community College of Philadelphia. **Stasis touch:** objects he touches stop in time and become immovable and invulnerable until he lets go (or for up to 90 seconds afterward). He freezes an armored car mid-turn on Broad Street in Episode 2. His mother needs dialysis and a transplant. He is Sam's dark mirror: a kid who thinks he has to save everyone, with nobody to tell him no. The press calls his crew **"the Freeze Frame Gang."**
  - **Bree Kowal** (ES-10), 18, Teddy's cousin. **Echo:** she can replay any sound she has heard, perfectly, and later any voice. She's funny and wants out.
  - **Hector "Heck" Ramos** (ES-22), 23, from Kensington, a former boxer. **Furnace:** strength that scales with body heat; he runs hot to the point of burning. Gentle until he isn't.
  - **Femi Ojo** (ES-31), 21, a Temple junior, Nigerian-American. **Unnoticeable:** attention slides off him. *Halcyon cleaners kill him in Episode 8.* This is the first real death of the show: brutal, sudden, and on the page.
- **The Iannucci family.** What's left of the Philly mob. **Bruno Iannucci** (68, from Passyunk, avuncular, terrifying) and his grandson **Dante Iannucci** (24, ambitious). Frank owes them $41,000 from his gambling days. Season 2's main antagonists. Teased in Season 1, Episode 7.
- **Halcyon Dynamics.** **Dr. Miriam Achterberg** (59, Dutch-American physicist, CEO, a true believer in Aperture). **Cole Brannigan** (45, head of "Recovery," ex-military, calm, polite, kills people). His team are "the cleaners." Seasons 1–3 and beyond.
- **The Revenant** (Old Sam). See §3.
- Later: the Pale Parade (Season 4 entity), federal Changed programs, international seam cartels, the Audience.

## 8. The Press-Name Gag

The press and the internet name the heroes before the heroes name themselves. The names get worse before they get better:
- **Pilot:** a blurry 9-second video shot outside the Wawa near campus (Broad & Cecil B. Moore, fictionalized) goes semi-viral as **"WAWA GUY."** Sam catches a phone thief faster than the camera's frame rate, and the thief is left holding a hoagie.
- **Episodes 2–4:** "the Broad Street Blur" (the Inquirer, metro reporter **Dana Fiorillo**) and "Septa Sonic" (Philly Twitter).
- **Later:** Sam eventually chooses his own name, as a player choice in Season 2–3. The ledger stores it. The press reacts to it.
- **Jules:** named after the Season 2 reveal. Candidates the press floats: "Lady Luck" (she hates it), "the Main Line Menace," "Odds." The player may choose for her in a Jules-POV episode.

## 9. Visual Evolution by Season (engine `look` themes)
- **Season 1, "Silver Age":** newsprint paper, CMYK process inks (cyan, magenta, yellow, key), Ben-Day halftone, and slight misregistration on titles. Bright, warm, funny. Night scenes are printed on ink-dark paper.
- **Season 2:** the same palette, inks heavier, more black.
- **Seasons 3–4, "Bronze Age":** muted earth tones and grit. Season 4 adds horror crosshatching.
- **Season 5:** painted, glossy, and modern.
- **Season 6, "Vertigo":** deep greens and blacks, painted grief.
- **Seasons 7–8, "Grid":** a strict nine-panel Watchmen grid that fractures over Season 8.
- **Seasons 9–16:** widescreen, then clear line, then a global blend.
- **Seasons 17–20:** Kirby crackle, psychedelic, Legion-surreal.
- **Seasons 21–23:** abstraction. The final episode returns to Season 1's newsprint.

## 10. Content Notes for Writers
- **Sex:** explicit, uncut, between adults, and always character-first: nerves, humor, specific bodies, consent shown through the characters' attention to each other, aftermath. The first time Sam and Jules sleep together should be one of the best-written scenes in the show. Other relationships (Simone, Nik, Grace, Vinnie, Callum, Jules with Callum) can also be on the page.
- **Violence:** graphic and consequential. Injuries persist in the engine. Powers make violence worse, not cleaner: time-stopped bodies, heat-burned flesh, the stasis-locked.
- **Grief:** given room. After a death, the next episode lives in it. Funerals, empty chairs, the dead person's phone still getting texts.
- **Horror:** specific and intimate. A Season 1 example: Femi's body found in a position that means he was still conscious when the cleaners worked.
- **Never:** sexual content involving minors (Gigi is 13 in Season 1; she ages normally, and nothing sexual is ever written involving her before adulthood; her later romance, if any, is adult-era only). No assault used as titillation.
