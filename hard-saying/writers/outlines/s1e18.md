half-moon sets ≈11 p.m.–midnight. **Time zones:** Ohio Eastern; Skye Pacific (−3); Lena Central (−1).

**Logline (spoiler-free):** *Thanksgiving on Glenwood Avenue: a grandmother with one good hand, a roommate in the empty chair, two hours without a phone — and a phone that won't stop
buzzing at the table.*

**Engine of the episode.** For four days Theo Vance — who has never been anybody's guest without a reservation — is taken into a family that has a hole in the middle of it, and
the family, without a word of discussion, fits him into the hole: Danny's bed, Danny's chair, a big brother for Gina, a pair of hands for a grandmother with a broken wrist, a
man in the garage for Frank to talk Lordstown to. Then on Friday Jack takes him to see the man whose place he's been keeping warm. Meanwhile, in Jack's pocket, a girl in Los
Angeles who is on television Thursdays at nine texts him through every meal, and Jack — who has just been handed the secret of who she is — keeps it, in his own house, from
everyone, including the roommate who knows her best, because she asked. **The episode is about what a family holds without saying.** Nonna says one sentence in Calabrese at a
hospital bed and asks for her slippers. Danny lies about a swan. Frank sits in the truck. Theo puts supermarket flowers at the feet of a plaster Madonna before dawn. Jack keeps a
girl in his pocket and a brother in his chest. And Lena calls from a loud house in New Orleans, and it's warm, and it's short, and he hangs up wanting more of it than either of them
has a right to.

**Register.** Warmth first (the curb; Maria's kitchen at nine at night; Nonna conscripting Theo; Rosie's prince; the table) — comedy all through (Gina, Theo's Fellini Italian, the
peach pie, the T9 against a girl who types like a hummingbird). The knife comes in three small cuts: Nonna's sentence (Thursday morning), the visiting room (Friday), the question
in the truck ("Does he ever say he's sorry?" — "No."). Thursday night is tender and a little guilty (Lena's voice; the buzz against his ear). Saturday night is heat, in words, in a
cold basement. Sunday is Advent: be vigilant; a wreath; Gina offering a hand across an aisle; a man in a garage telling a rich boy to come back; a twenty pressed into a palm.

**Cadence check.** **Two-hander with Lena: none** — the brief's newest note (E18: in touch regularly, not as deeply as Christmas) supersedes the bible's "E18: the Thanksgiving call
as the two-hander"; the call is a warm ≈1,300-word phone scene, the Broussards loud, Lena alone for three minutes, then gone. The every-other cadence resumes at **E20** (the courtyard;
restraint #2) — inside the brief's "room may shift by one." **Ensemble setpiece:** the Glenwood Thanksgiving (family, not campus ensemble; s05–s06). **Duel:** none (the Brideshead
exchange at Trumbull is conversation, unscored). Campus ensemble by phone and text only (Laney via Theo; Kevin, Margot one text each; Becca one text).

**Length:** **≈20,500 words per path (ceiling ~22,000; Skye exception).** **19 choices per path** (20 on the basement branch); 9–10 keys; 6 cues; 5 needle-drops; **2 genuine
forks** (`e18_night` Thursday; `e18_sat` Saturday) — both converge.
