## 1. SHAPE

**Title:** **"A Good Man"** (O'Connor's title, cut in half). Becca was one and it wasn't the thing; Julian is one and it may not be enough; Margot says so; the Misfit
says there's no real pleasure in life; the grandmother, at the end, calls a murderer her child; and Jack — the brief's good man who may not believe he is — spends two
days being miserable in a decent man's disguise, and one night being exactly as good as he wants to be, and wakes up with a secret.

**Dates:** **Thursday, November 19 – Saturday, November 21, 2009** (tag ≈5:45 p.m. Sat). Karzai sworn in; the UC Regents' fee vote at UCLA (Thu — TV, not Marisol);
***New Moon* midnight shows Thu → Fri** (record $26.3M midnight [V]); the Wheeler Hall occupation at Berkeley (Fri — TV); **the Manhattan Declaration** (Fri; online
signing that day — recalled); *The Blind Side* opens (Fri); Presentation of Mary (Sat); **Ohio State 21, Michigan 10, Ann Arbor, noon ET = 9:00 a.m. PST [V]** — the sixth
straight (2004–09); Senate cloture 60–39 (Sat night — not used). Saints idle (9–0). Moon: a thin crescent sets by ~8:30 p.m. Thu and Fri — **no moon after midnight**;
the Leonids are done. Sunrise Sat ≈6:33; first gray ≈5:45. Dry, clear, mild; cool nights (climatology only — **no rain beat**).

**Logline (spoiler-free; in the header):** *Two bad days, a piano nobody admits to playing, a town full of vampires — and a party in Montecito where somebody has Flannery
O'Connor exactly backwards.*

**Engine of the episode.** E15 was a decent man doing the hard thing. E16 is what it costs him, which nobody is allowed to see: he tells everyone it's Becca, and
the two people who love him most and aren't in love with him know better in one look. At the Thursday 12:10 Lena has saved him the end of a pew, as she said she
would, and across the aisle Becca turns at the sign of peace and holds out her hand. Thursday night Lena is warmer than she has ever been — a basement, a book, a
piece of music with a hole in it — and at 11:40 Julian is at the top of the stairs. Friday she has two soups and keeps the one Julian brought; Laney tries to tell her
the truth through a door and Lena won't hear it; Margot tells Jack the truth on the stairs and means it. So Theo does the only thing he knows: he puts Jack in a borrowed
shirt and takes him an hour down the 101 to a house in Montecito *not* to meet anyone — and loses him in three minutes. And Jack, for the first time in his life, crosses a room on his own.
For one long night a stranger looks at him like nothing else exists, argues him into the best thing he believes, says what she wants out loud, and leaves on her own timing.
The cost isn't the kiss. It's 6:05 a.m., a roommate in a camel coat asking where he went, and Jack's first lie of omission — and then, at halftime, the invitation that's
been in his mouth for two weeks finally coming out, to Theo, who is too confused to do anything but say yes.
