**Logline (spoiler-free):** *Two days in January: an empty shelf, a disaster on a car radio, and a card table on the Commons Lawn where everybody meets
everybody.*

**Engine of the episode.** Tuesday morning Jack puts his mother's empty coffee can up on the top shelf so he won't have to look at it, and there's
nothing beside it where a black bag used to be. He looks, quietly, and tells nobody. Lena at the mailboxes is pleasant to him for the second day in her
life; she tells him the baptism was lovely, and asks how his break was, and he gives her everything but the last night. At one Skye takes him to an
empty lot to teach him to drive her car, and charges him a kiss a stall, and argues that nobody in her business can promise anybody anything — which
is why Sunday mattered — and at 2:31 the radio says a city has fallen down. By dark the Fishbowl TV is all Haiti; Theo asks Kevin how long a person can
last under a building and goes to his rush party anyway. At eight St. Brendan's is full, and Lena has a clipboard and a schedule and the whisper her
mother gives orders in, and puts Jack on the table Wednesday with Becca. When the TV in the Newman lounge says *trapped*, Lena leaves the room, and
Julian goes after her, and whether Jack follows or not, he sees what Julian does — he sits, and doesn't talk, and doesn't ask. At midnight, alone at the
Fishbowl computer, Jack finds the dead girl's memorial page and the jacket on her back. Wednesday Osei says Mass for an archbishop found in rubble, and
says everybody in Port-au-Prince is calling a name into the dark. On the Commons Becca tells him about her grandmother's rosary at a mine gate as if
it were a sweet story; then Skye turns up, borrows Becca's apron, and raises more money than the Knights of Columbus by being Cassidy for twenty dollars
a name. Between customers she asks him where God was at 4:53, and they go at it the way he used to go at it with somebody else — and that somebody
crosses the lawn with Laney at three, and Laney says the line, loud enough, and goes and does it. Skye says *You're Lena*. Lena tells her where the
checks go. Theo is polite to Skye and rude to Julian; Laney is sweet to Julian and poison to Skye; Jack says nothing. That night Skye fights her
publicist on the phone with his hand in hers, and then — in a dark church lit for Haiti, or on her porch — asks him to say a prayer for them "from me,"
and asks who everybody at the table was, and he names them all but one. At the front desk Lena is locking up the money; she tells him the number,
and that Skye's envelope was the biggest, and says *Tell her thank you. From Newman.* Upstairs a whiteboard spins too fast. In the dark, the can is on
the shelf where the bag was.

**Register.** Warmth first even on a disaster day: a stick-shift lesson; Theo's raw onion; a chapel full on a Tuesday; Becca's crab and Becca's
grandmother; Skye in an apron selling a fictional girl's voice for real money; Laney's bells; Margot's notebook. **The knife:** the shelf; "It was
lovely."; "Then bring her."; Julian on the back steps; the memorial page; "neither one was Walt"; "a California you"; "You're Lena."; "One."; "Tell her
thank you." **The darkness is real and offstage** (Port-au-Prince; Lena's grandmother's chair) — **never decoration**: Haiti is not a backdrop for
romance; every scene that touches it gives it its weight first (the radio, the chapel, the rescue footage, Osei's friend, the archbishop).
