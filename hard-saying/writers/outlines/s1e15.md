## 1. SHAPE

**Logline (spoiler-free):** *A bill comes due, home calls at one in the morning, somebody stays to the end of a song — and Christmas comes up early.*

**Engine of the episode.** E14 was Jack being there for her. E15 opens with Lena being there for him — his father, his sister, his money — in the
same week she is being asked, gently, by a better-mannered man than Jack has ever been. At noon Friday, under the eucalyptus, she mentions it on
the way to something else. Jack smiles and says the right thing, and spends a weekend being excellent company; the people who love him and aren't
in love with him can tell. Then his mother writes to Becca, and Becca shines, and at two in the morning, with a jar of her mother's jam in his
hand, Jack understands that the reason he'd been waiting is gone and the truth about Becca was always the same. He tells her the truth in her
brother's truck. That night Lena comes up to the lounge in her socks and says every right thing for ten minutes, and goes back downstairs, and
Laney's face in a doorway is the only place on the page the cost shows.

**Register.** Comedy front-loaded (the Wall duel; Theo's Hasselhoff; Laney's open-mic scorecards; FOR THE CATHOLIC ROOMMATE); dread in the middle
(one in the morning on the landing; Gina's text; the Folgers can); a Thursday that turns on one line ("Can I walk you back?"); a Friday that turns
on one aside; a very funny weekend (the worst part); a St. Jude card at a Browns shutout; a planner open to December; the kindest breakup in the
show; ten right minutes; a door.

**Cadence check.** **Two-hander: none owed** (E14 had it; **E16 carries it** — the Thursday basement piano, the night after this episode ends). Lena
