**Logline (spoiler-free):** *A correction sent after a morning of drafts, a reply that takes all day, a grandmother on the stairs — and coffee at an hour nobody keeps, the night before
everyone goes home.*

**Engine of the episode.** E16 was the first thing that happened to Jack that nobody arranged. E17 is what he does with it, which is mostly wait — and then, for the first time, say so.
He spends a Sunday morning of 10–0 and a Lions miracle writing a correction on T9 to a girl who had O'Connor backwards; sends it; and hears nothing through Christ the King, a courtyard
where Lena asks one question too many, and the long dark — until 11:48, when the phone lights up and a stranger makes him laugh alone in a stairwell. Monday his grandmother falls and he
tells his mother the truth about Becca. Tuesday the floor goes home, the 12:10 reads Nonna's name aloud, and a girl texts that her Swedish car is an orphan and it's his fault. At one in
the morning she's waiting past the Wharf Rat. Nearly three hours at the Starlite by the freeway: she won't say what she's in, so she makes him play a Bible salesman instead; she asks what
believing is *like*; she asks about his grandmother and refuses to do her voice; she says she wants him to miss her. A kiss in a parking lot that is nothing like the first one, slower, on
purpose. She leaves. He sits on a curb with a pie for Nonna on his knees and thinks about her mouth, and is still thinking about it on the 101, at the airport between two holy cards, and in
seat 14A over the mountains. Then, at a gate in the middle of the country, an email gives him the last name she wouldn't, and the last name gives him everything else.

**Register.** Sunday comic and tender (the drafts; Dale's joy; the glass), a long held breath (Mass; the wall; the dark), a laugh in a stairwell. Monday the floor drops out (Gina; Maria
in her coat; "Oh, honey"). Tuesday a sweet scattering. **The Starlite is the episode's long held note** — four movements, warm, funny, specific, hours of it — and the lot is heat.
Wednesday guilty and luminous and very tired, and then, at the gate, the floor drops out a second time, comically: he explained GM to a girl who's on television.
