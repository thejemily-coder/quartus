**Logline (spoiler-free; in the header):** *A correction sent after a morning of drafts, a reply that takes all day, a grandmother on the stairs — and coffee at an hour nobody keeps, the night before everyone
goes home.*

**Engine of the episode.** E16 was the first thing that happened to Jack that nobody arranged. E17 is what he does with it, which is mostly wait — and then, for the first time, say so. He spends a Sunday
morning of 10–0 and a Lions miracle writing a correction on T9 to a girl who had O'Connor backwards; sends it; hears nothing through Christ the King and a courtyard where Lena asks one question too many —
until 11:48, when the phone lights up in a quiet lounge and a stranger rewrites the Misfit at him, and he laughs so hard that four friends and the girl he can't have all look up, and he can't tell them why.
Monday his grandmother falls and he tells his mother the truth about Becca. Tuesday the floor goes home, the 12:10 reads Nonna's name aloud, and a girl texts that her Swedish car is an orphan and it's his
fault. At one in the morning she's waiting past the Wharf Rat, and she drives twenty minutes up the 101 to a truck stop nobody she knows has ever been to. Nearly three hours: she won't say what she's in, so
she casts him as a Bible salesman instead; she asks about his grandmother and refuses to do her voice; she asks what believing is *like*; she says she wants him to miss her. A kiss in a parking lot that is
nothing like the first one — slower, on purpose. She leaves. He sits on a curb with a pie for Nonna on his knees and thinks about her mouth, and is still thinking about it on the 101, at the airport between two
holy cards, and in seat 14A over the mountains. Then, on the floor of an airport in the middle of the country, she gives him her last name — and his sister gives him the rest.

**Register.** Sunday comic and tender (the drafts; Dale's joy; the glass), a long held breath (Mass; the wall), and a laugh that breaks a quiet room. Monday the floor drops out (Gina; Maria in her coat; "Oh,
honey"). Tuesday a sweet scattering. **The Starlite is the episode's long held note** — four movements, warm, funny, specific, hours of it — and the lot is heat. Wednesday guilty and luminous and very tired,
and then, on a concourse carpet, the floor drops out a second time, comically, down a phone line: he explained GM to a girl who's on television.

**Cadence check.** **Two-hander with Lena: none** (E16 carried it; E18's is a short Thanksgiving call by the player's design; the cadence resumes at E20). The long two-person scene is **Jack and Skye** (the
Starlite). Lena carries eight real beats. **Setpiece:** none (the floor scattering is a montage). **Duel:** none (the Starlite's "Good Country People" scene is improv, unscored; Hale's record card untouched).

**Length:** **≈21,650 words per path (ceiling ~22,000; Skye exception)**; the Starlite and the lot (s16–s21) ≈7,900 of it; the hub (s24–s24b) ≈2,000. **22 choices per path**; 12 keys; 6 cues; 4 needle-drops.

**Format of a choice line:** `[Tag] "Text"` · **if** gate · **set** `{…}` · **cue** · **key** · → goto · *response gist*. Quoted text is the on-screen choice text (writers may polish wording, never meaning).
Every `set` uses schema variables only; `rel.*` only on tracked ids (`lena theo laney margot kevin osei hale julian evan becca wes skye`) — **never** `rel.frank/gina/maria/nonna/gloria/rudy/dale/bev`.
**Increments:** traits and soul `"+1"` per choice (±2 only where marked); `rel.*` ±1 (±2 marked); `resolve.jack` as `"+1"`/`"-1"` strings; **`resolve.lena` absolute numbers, scene `set` only.**
**`rel.becca.*` is never touched. `rel.lena.heat` is never touched (cap 0).**
**Conditionals.** In-episode branches are always gated. **Every E16 reference is gated on every value** (an `else`, or an exhaustive set of gates — §9.2). Save-canon E15-and-earlier callbacks are gated on their
flag with **no `else`** where absence reads fine (the save satisfies them; the gate keeps the file honest). Flags set on **every** E16 path (§9.2, first row) may be written unconditionally. Response arrays may carry
`{ "if", "text", "else" }` items exactly like bodies. **The engine applies a choice's `set` before it renders the response** — so a response may read a flag the same choice just set; the `said_it_plain_n` gates below
rely on this.
**Scene splits for the engine.** Choices sit only at the end of a scene, so beats that must follow a choice on every path get a short continuation scene (s03a, s07a, s18a, s20k, s24a, s24b) or a shared tail written
into each response (marked **Tail (every response)** — the writer writes it once and copies it; keep it short).
**Texts.** SMS inserts: `{"type":"sms","from":"…","text":"…","time":"…"}`. Senders as Jack's RAZR shows them: **SKYE (MISFIT)**, **Lena**, **Gina**, **Becca**; Jack's own sent texts `from: "Jack"`. Skye: lowercase,
fast, no apostrophes, caps for emphasis. Jack: lowercase T9, short, each word a labor. **Lena: full sentences, punctuation, signed —L.** (LG enV2 keyboard; E14 "Ask me again in a week. —L."). Gina: lowercase.
**No typing indicators, read receipts, iMessage, front cameras, "selfie."**
