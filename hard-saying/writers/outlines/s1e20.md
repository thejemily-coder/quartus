## 1. SHAPE

**Title:** **"Stay."** Four people ask Jack Costigan to stay this week, and each means something different. **Julian**, Sunday, in the courtyard with two coffees: *Will you stay to the end?* **Lena**, Wednesday at the Brick, owning her
own old sin with a smile: *Whatever you do, don't leave in the second chorus.* **Skye**, Thursday night, plainly, on her bed: *Stay.* — the one he says no to. And Skye at dawn: *You said no. And you're still here.* — because he did
the thing nobody has ever done to her: he said no and **stayed**. (Echoes Yusuf's E19 "If you're going to come, stay." Never said in the narration except where a character says it.) Under it, unspoken, the other half of the
title: the sentence he says at three in the morning that leaves the door **ajar** — *a no right now* — and her "I can work with that."

**Logline (spoiler-free):** *Twelve and oh in overtime, a rainstorm indoors and out, a holy day on three hours' sleep, a song with a name in it — and a door somebody has never opened for anyone.*

**Engine of the episode.** Jack Costigan spends a week being asked to stay. On Sunday, after a game won by a missed chip shot, Julian asks him to stay to the end of a song he's finally finished, and Jack promises. On Monday he's in
Burbank in the first storm of the winter, somebody's cousin from Ohio standing under a rain tower while the real rain beats on the stage roof, watching Skye be someone's preacher's daughter — and telling her, because he can't help
it, that nobody forgives anybody in the rain; you forgive them the next morning, when it's dry and it costs something. She plays it that way. At four in the morning, home from her world, he catches the culprit of five fire alarms in
the act — and can't explain to anyone why he was in the hall to see it. On the feast of a girl who was full of grace before she'd done a single thing, he kneels at noon, or behind Lena and Julian at dusk while a picture buzzes during
the Ave, or sleeps through it; that night, in a walk-in cooler, he opens the picture, and at one in the morning Skye calls him from a laundromat where she is trying to make a borrowed dress look like it went to the cleaners, and
gets the feast wrong, and when he tells her what it means she says *Must be nice.* On Wednesday Lena finds him at the Brick so they can choose each other's best arguments, and says only: *He played it for me. It's good. Come
Thursday. Don't leave in the second chorus.* On Thursday, in daylight, Skye comes out of a math test where for two hours nobody looked at her once, and is happier than he has seen her; that night Julian sings "Magdalena," and
Lena watches Jack hear it. It's genuinely good, which is the worst part. Jack walks out onto Mission Street and calls the one person who will ask him nothing, and she doesn't, and she takes him home — the first person she ever
has — and on her bed, with nothing on the clock for once, she asks him plainly to stay. He says no. Everything stops, because she needs to understand what just happened. **He stays**, and tells her why till the window goes gray —
and when she asks, in the only voice she has that isn't a voice, whether he thinks she's a slut, he puts his arms around her before he answers, and feels her go stiff, and says *no*, and tells her the truth: the jealousy is his;
the teaching is for judging himself. *You're the only person who's ever told me no and meant it.* And then, because he is honest and wants to be kind, he tells her he isn't promising it will always be no — just that it's no
**right now** — and she says, *I can work with that.* **The episode is about the difference between a no that leaves and a no that stays — and how a good man, telling the truth, leaves a door open.** Lena's text from 11:41 sits
