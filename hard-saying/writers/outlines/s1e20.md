## 1. SHAPE

**Title:** **"Stay."** Four people ask Jack Costigan to stay this week, and each means something different. **Julian**, Sunday, in the courtyard with two coffees: *Will you stay to the end?* (E15's promise,
renewed for the song). **Lena**, Wednesday at the Brick, owning her own old sin with a smile: *Whatever you do, don't leave in the second chorus.* **Skye**, Thursday night, plainly, on her bed: *Stay.* — the
one he says no to. And Skye again at dawn, after: *You said no. And you're still here.* — because he did the thing nobody has ever done to her: he said no and **stayed**. Echoes Yusuf's E19 "If you're going to
come, stay." Never said in the narration except where a character says it.

**Dates:** **Sunday, December 6 – Friday, December 11, 2009 (dawn).**

  strip-and-score of an interception before the half; Suisham's missed short field goal late; Brees to Meachem to tie; Hartley in OT — Researcher confirms every detail used]. Browns at Chargers [V: L 23–30;
  "One and eleven"]. Maria's Trumbull visit ("Sunday week," E18).

**Logline (spoiler-free):** *Twelve and oh in overtime, a rainstorm indoors and out, a holy day on three hours' sleep, a song with a name in it — and a door somebody has never opened for anyone.*

**Engine of the episode.** Jack Costigan spends a week being asked to stay. On Sunday, after a game won by a missed chip shot, Julian asks him to stay to the end of a song he's finally finished, and Jack
promises. On Monday he's in Burbank in the first storm of the winter, somebody's cousin from Ohio standing in fake rain under a rain tower while the real rain beats on the stage roof, watching Skye be
someone's preacher's daughter — and telling her, because he can't help it, that nobody forgives anybody in the rain; you forgive them the next morning, when it's dry and it costs something. She plays it
that way. The take is better. At four in the morning, home from her world, he catches the culprit of five fire alarms in the act — and can't explain to anyone why he was in the hall to see it. On the feast of a
girl who was full of grace before she'd done a single thing, he either kneels beside Kevin at noon, or behind Lena and Julian at dusk while a picture buzzes in his pocket during the Ave Maria, or sleeps through
it; that night, in a walk-in cooler, he opens the picture. On Wednesday Lena finds him at the Brick so they can choose each other's best arguments for Hale's oral, and she has the look of someone who has been
given something too big, and she says only: *He played it for me. It's good. Come Thursday. Don't leave in the second chorus.* On Thursday Julian sings "Magdalena," and Lena watches Jack hear it. It's
genuinely good, which is the worst part. Jack walks out onto Mission Street and calls the one person who will ask him nothing, and she doesn't, and she takes him home — the first person she ever has —
and on her bed, with nothing on the clock for once, she asks him plainly to stay. He says no. Everything stops, because she needs to understand what just happened. And then — instead of leaving, the way
everyone leaves her after a no or a yes — **he stays**, and tells her why, all of it, till the window goes gray: what a body says; what a promise is; something that happened when he was sixteen. And she
goes quiet, which she never does, and asks him the thing she's scared of. **The episode is about the difference between a no that leaves and a no that stays.** Lena's text from 11:41 sits unread in a
switched-off phone on a stranger's floor until dawn.

**Register.** Warmth first: Sully's in overtime; Theo's blanket; Maria reading a phone bill like scripture; Danny's "Who's Flannery O'Connor?" relayed by a mother who doesn't know; Burbank's crew, the rain

s17 the furthest yet), each with character. The knife in small cuts: "Will you stay to the end?"; Lena's Book Nine text read at video village; the buzz at the Ave; "He played it for me"; the face-up phone;
"Magdalena"; the RAZR switched off in the Saab. **Darkly funny while sad** (brief), then, at the end, simply tender — the most serious Skye has been, and the most herself.
