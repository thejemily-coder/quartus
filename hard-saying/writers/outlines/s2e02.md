## 1. SHAPE

**Title:** **"Holy Family."** Holy Family Sunday at St. Brigid's and in a visiting room at Trumbull; Luke 2:48 — *"Your father and I have been looking for you"* — a boy missing three days found in the place he belongs; the fifth Joyful Mystery
he started on his knuckles on the steps of St. Brendan's (E22 s06b) and Nonna's beads on Monday; the Broussards and a musician at their table; a girl in a childhood bedroom under the call sheets of the family she was raised by. Never said as
a theme.

**Dates:** **Saturday, December 26 – Thursday, December 31, 2009** (the tag runs to ≈3:20 a.m. EST Fri Jan 1). Time zones: Youngstown Eastern; New Orleans −1; LA −3.
- **Sat Dec 26** — St. Stephen. The Christmas rain froze overnight [V]; Frank salting the drive. Theo landed after midnight. The *Sweetwater* marathon in the front room (≈2 p.m.). *Orthodoxy* by text at midnight.

  **Saints–Bucs** [V kickoff ET] arrives in Lena's texts — **20–17 Tampa, in overtime**; **11:10 p.m. Lena's long call** (Danny's room).

  **Carly** (both paths); **1:00 a.m. EST Lena's midnight** from the river; **3:00 a.m. EST Skye's midnight** on Facebook chat (the basement Dell).

**Logline (spoiler-free):** *The week after Christmas on Glenwood Avenue: a television show nobody in the family can stop watching, a father who finally gets out of the truck, a phone call at an hour nobody keeps — and midnight three times.*

**Engine of the episode.** The day after Christmas the rain has frozen on Glenwood Avenue and Frank, chipping the drive, asks his son what it's like in there now — "the room" — and the player hears for the first time that Frank has never
seen it. Gina puts the second season of *Sweetwater* in the DVD player for research, and Jack, who doesn't watch television, watches the girl from the turnout be sixteen and good and kind on a dock in a blazer with shoulder pads while
his grandmother prays out loud for Cassidy, and his mother asks which one she is really. At midnight the girl herself is reading Chesterton in Studio City because it was in his back pocket, and has a question about the Ethics of Elfland:
who picked midnight. On Holy Family Sunday Fr. Dom reads the short Colossians (Jack texts New Orleans; New Orleans read the long one — *We are not discussing it*), and in the lot at Trumbull his father turns the engine off for the first
time in two years and asks where he's supposed to stand. Danny stands up when he sees him. Danny laughs at the TV girl till the officer looks over and says nothing else, in front of anyone; and while their mother teaches their father
how the quarters work, Danny says one sentence to Jack about a truck. The Browns win their third straight on the garage radio and the Saints lose in overtime in Lena's texts — *They're resting.* — and that night, in his brother's room,
Jack tells Lena his father went in, the way he used to bring her his father, and she listens to all of it and then has to go: Julian calls at ten his time. Monday his grandmother prays for the nun and for Cassidy, and he can't make her
say *Skye*. And at half past one Tuesday morning, two floors under his family, a girl in her childhood bed under a corkboard of old call sheets finishes the argument about the cow and the palace, and says *Monday. Not asking. Telling.* —
and tells him, slowly and exactly, what she is going to do the night he doesn't say no; and he doesn't stop her, because he can't say yes, and this is the only yes there is; and then she asks for his, and he gives it to her, badly, and she
likes it; and she says she has never said any of it before, only done it; and when it passes he sits on the bottom step and is afraid of himself. In the morning she texts that she finished his Chesterton at four, and he grins at the
kitchen table, and both of those are true. Tuesday Lena calls from her father's fish market to say *home field*, and her father yells that he's making a gumbo for the musician. Wednesday the picture comes into his father's hand, and his
father gives it back shut, and nothing comes from New Orleans all day. New Year's Eve the Costigans do midnight at nine for Rosie, Frank toasts with a ginger ale, and Jack goes to a party full of people who knew him before, or stays home —
and either way Carly Benedetto finds him a little after midnight and asks if he ever hears how Mikey's mom is doing. At one his phone has a river on fire in it and Julian beside it. At three a girl in a bathroom at a party in the hills types
that she watched the ball drop on the East Coast feed at nine so she'd be at midnight with him, and then midnight comes for her too. **The episode is about who goes in and who waits outside — a father, a son, a boy who has stopped saying no
to a voice on a phone — and the one sentence about it Jack is given and can't use yet.**

**Register.** Warmth first almost everywhere: ice and salt, the family helpless in front of a teen soap, Nonna's rosary for a fictional girl, Rosie's grace over vending-machine honey buns, Danny's laugh, Frank getting the coffee wrong, Tony

moralized and never staged as a punishment. Bittersweet comedy everywhere else — the proxy war at its last gentle moment before January.
