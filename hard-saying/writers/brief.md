# THE HARD SAYING — Creative Brief (binding)

This is the player's brief, assembled from a long design interview. Everything here is a decision the player made. It is
NOT secret: the player wrote it. Hidden canon lives in `bible.md`. Where the bible and this brief conflict, the brief wins.

## The show
- **Format:** A text-based, choice-driven game presented as a prestige-television college drama, played in episodes and
  seasons. Each episode is a full TV hour: cold open, acts, tag. **12,000–18,000 words**, 15–25 meaningful choices.
- **Title:** *The Hard Saying* (John 6:60: "This is a hard saying; who can listen to it?").
- **Structure:** 1 season = 1 semester, **22 episodes**, plus an abbreviated **summer season** between school years.
  The run covers four years of college and continues into adulthood after graduation.
- **Era:** Starts **Fall 2009** (move-in late August 2009) and moves forward through real history. Real events, politics,
  people in the news, pop culture, and Church events (Benedict XVI, *Caritas in Veritate*, the health-care fight, the
  recession, H1N1, Facebook, flip phones and T9, later the 2012 election, Benedict's resignation and Francis's election
  in 2013) stay in the background and shape conversations. Get them right; the liturgical calendar is real.
- **Influences:** Hart of Dixie, Gilmore Girls, Greek, Euphoria, Entourage, Task, Red Dead Redemption 2, The Witcher 3,
  The Last of Us, the Cormoran Strike novels.
- **Tone:** *Warmth first, knife second.* A blend of sweetness, warmth, comedy, heat, tension, darkness, grief and pain.
  Mostly Gilmore/Hart of Dixie warmth and banter so that the darkness lands harder when it comes.
- **Faith lens:** *True, and hard.* The story treats Catholicism as true and serious: grace is real, sin has weight. The
  faith is never easy, and its critics get their best arguments. Flannery O'Connor, not a youth-group pamphlet.

## The protagonist: Jack Costigan (the player)
- Freshman (18) from **Youngstown, Ohio**: Rust Belt Irish/Italian, working-class, parish school, first in his family at
  a big university. Devout Catholic. Philosophy major.
- **Charming, restless, reckless.** Magnetic, funny, a little dangerous. His faith is real, and so is the pull of the
  world. Arthur Morgan-adjacent: a good man who may not believe he is.
- Well-read, bright, loves philosophy and theology. Argues for sport and for keeps.
- **POV: Jack only.** The player is Jack. Lena stays a mystery read through her words and actions. Never show a scene
  Jack isn't in. Never narrate another character's thoughts as fact.
- **Narration: close third person, present tense.** ("Jack watches her cross the porch.")
- His family appears on-screen: phone calls, holidays, trouble at home.

## The love interest: Magdalena "Lena" Broussard
- Freshman (18) from **New Orleans**. Southern Catholic culture: big family, crawfish boils, Mardi Gras and Lent, faith
  as heritage and food and noise. Classics/philosophy student, so she is **Jack's direct rival** in the same seminars,
  competing for the same mentor's approval.
- **Fierce and incandescent.** Quick, combative, funny, impossible to out-talk. Loves a fight and wins most of them.
  Underneath is a tenderness she guards ferociously. Lorelai crossed with Catherine of Siena. Beautiful and magnetic.
- The Broussards appear on-screen: Thanksgiving and Mardi Gras episodes; loud, close, complicated.

## The romance: the central arc
- **Endgame, earned.** They end up together, but it costs them: breakups, other people, at least one devastating season.
- **Slow burn**, focused on heat, tension and emotion.
- **They meet** at a loud Saturday-night party, both dragged there by new friends and both hesitant: the only two sober-ish
  people on the back porch, judging everyone, then each other. **They collide again at Mass the next morning.**
- **The cultural difference, stated precisely:** neither is puritanical. Neither is against drinking, fun, music, dancing
  or flirting. What they were raised against is drinking to destroy the ability to reason, drugs, and the disposability
  of hook-up culture. **Never portray either of them as prudish, preachy, or scandalized by fun.**
- **What keeps them apart:**
  - **She's taken** when the game opens. The writers' room decides who he is; the player wants to discover it.
  - **Secrets and wounds.** Each carries something they haven't told anyone. Allowed: family rupture, past relationship
    harm, and anything else that serves the story (grief, abuse, illness, crime). These are revealed over time and kept
    from the player until they land.
  - **Same faith, different conclusions.** Both are traditionalists to an extent but apply it differently to facts and
    circumstances (politics, people, the Church), which produces regular, heated, *enjoyable* arguments.
  - **Other relationships.** Before they get together, both have several relationships that don't work out, mostly
    because the other person wants something completely different from a college relationship. But each of them (at
    different times) has a partner who **really tries** to respect and understand where they're coming from, so those
    endings are sad rather than simply expected. For Lena, that is **Julian Park-Ellis** (sophomore musician, secular,
    gentle, heartbreakingly decent). For Jack, the "tryer" is a separate character from the magnetic girl who pulls him
    away; the writers build both.
- **Their arguments** run through the whole series: **Aquinas vs. Augustine** (reason and grace, nature and fallenness);
  **politics and the Church**; **literature as moral test** (Dostoevsky, O'Connor, Waugh, Tolkien vs. Lewis, Percy,
  Greene); **how to love the secular** (withdraw, engage or convert). The arguments are part of the spark. Both of them
  are brilliant. Lena wins about as often as Jack does.
- **Physical heat:** *restraint is the fire* for a long time, probably several seasons, then a slow, gradual erosion.
  How far it goes, and whether they hold the line until marriage, is **ultimately the culmination of the player's
  choices**. Giving in before marriage won't destroy them, but it brings serious guilt, rupture and repair.
  **They trade off which one of them is able to hold**, and they are in real trouble on a night when neither can.
  (Mechanically: the resolve system below.)

## The ensemble (tight core of about 8 with deep arcs, plus a drifting wider cast)
- **Theo Vance**, Jack's roommate. Son of an LA studio exec: silver-tongued, gorgeous, exhausting, a rich hedonist best
  friend. Loves Jack and is constantly tempting him. Hidden depths and a dark arc. "Theodore" when he's in trouble.
- **Delaney "Laney" Price**, Lena's roommate and best friend. Dallas, a former pageant girl, blinding smile, a Xanax
  prescription. Wild-child sorority rusher at a top house; she dragged Lena to the party. Fiercely loyal and
  self-destructive, with a Euphoria-shaded arc. Their friendship is a love story of its own.
- **Margot Lindqvist**, a militant feminist atheist in their dorm. Portland, raised by two academics, gender-studies
  major, clove cigarettes. For reasons unclear to any of them she becomes one of Jack and Lena's good mutual friends
  and stays one through college, with many a heated argument. She regularly accuses Lena of internalized misogyny, Jack
  of upholding the patriarchy, and both of them of being useful idiots for Christian nationalism. Never a strawman.
- **Fr. Gabriel Osei**, the Newman Center chaplain. Ghanaian-born, mid-40s, a former physician. Warm, unflappable, sees
  through everyone. A priest with a past.
- **Prof. Adrian Hale**, the atheist philosophy professor who mentors both of them. British, 50s, a celebrity public
  intellectual: Hitchens' swagger, Bernard Williams' depth. Drinks too much. Loves them both, maybe too much. He and
  Fr. Osei pull them in opposite directions.
- **Kevin Tran**, the gay Catholic friend. San Jose, Vietnamese Catholic family, pre-med, an iron-clad public persona.
  Out to Margot and no one else. Caught between faith and identity, written with compassion and complexity and no easy
  answers either way.
- **Julian Park-Ellis**, Lena's rival suitor (see above).
- **A magnetic girl for Jack** who pulls him away from Lena, and separately **the girl who truly tries**. Writers build both.
- **Recurring supporting players:** an overwhelmed junior **RA**; the floor's discreet weed and Adderall **dealer**; a
  **TA** with secrets. Plus Jack's family (Youngstown) and Lena's family (New Orleans).
- Supporting characters can drift into the core over time.

## Looks (the player chose these)
- **Lena:** sun-streaked honey-brown waves, golden skin, warm amber-brown eyes; classic Gulf Coast beauty. About 5'5",
  soft and curvy, at peace with her body, neither hiding nor flaunting it; warmth in every line. Wears her
  grandmother's worn gold **Miraculous Medal** on a thin chain and touches it when she's thinking or lying. Thrifted
  **sundresses with beat-up cowboy boots** or Keds, a cardigan when the Marisol fog rolls in. Sometimes veils at Mass
  in a **black lace mantilla**, which draws looks even at Newman.
- **Jack:** 6'0", black hair that needs cutting, pale skin, blue eyes, a nose broken once in a CYO basketball fight,
  stubble by noon. Wears a **St. Michael medal** from his confirmation, and has a **scar through one eyebrow** whose
  story he tells differently every time. Always a battered **paperback in his back pocket** (Chesterton, Camus,
  Percy), spine cracked, margins full. Smokes **Marlboro Reds** when stressed or drinking, and is "quitting"; it's a
  whole thing with Lena.
- **Theo:** golden LA prince. Tall, tanned, artfully messy blond hair, green eyes, very expensive clothes worn like
  they're cheap. Looks like a Calvin Klein ad and knows it.
- **Laney:** redhead bombshell. Copper hair, porcelain skin, freckles she covers, a laugh you can hear across a party.
  Petite, all hips and attitude.
- **Margot:** severe Scandinavian. White-blonde hair cut blunt at the jaw, very pale, thick black-framed glasses, all
  black clothing, Doc Martens. Seems to have no body temperature.
- **Kevin:** gym-built pre-med. Compact and muscular, a crew cut, an athletic quarter-zip, a big easy smile that never
  quite reaches his eyes.
- **Fr. Osei:** gentle and weathered. Medium height, thin, a graying beard, deep laugh lines, a slight limp he never explains.
- **Prof. Hale:** rumpled Oxford rake. Tall, silver hair swept back too long, a linen jacket with wine on the cuff, a
  once-beautiful face gone ruddy and broken-veined. Still devastating when he smiles.
- **Julian:** quietly handsome. Medium height, clean-cut, warm brown skin, short dark hair, a calm presence; you'd
  trust him with anything. Musician.

## Setting
- **California State University at Marisol ("Marisol")**, a fictional coastal elite public university: a beach-town
  flagship with sun, surf and a ferocious academic reputation. Intensely secular and progressive, high-achieving and
  cutthroat. Being openly Catholic is countercultural there. Catholics are a clear minority, of varying belief.
- **Jack and Lena live in the same co-ed freshman dorm** on different floors, sharing the lounge, the laundry and the
  3 a.m. fire drills. Unbearable proximity.
- **The Newman Center** is home base and battleground, with its own cast (Fr. Osei, missionaries, earnest kids,
  hypocrites, perhaps a rival couple). The faith community is as dramatic as anywhere else.
- Friendships and relationships are consistently tested by the faith-versus-culture divide.

## Story architecture
- Mysteries, twists and reveals are kept from the player. The player may direct some things in notes sessions; never
  spoil what's hidden.
- **Arc durations:** some storylines last one episode, some several episodes, some a season, some several seasons, and
  some the entire run. Keep a registry (in the bible) and pay them off on schedule.
- **Central mystery:** the player chose "surprise me completely" and doesn't want to know even its flavor.
- **One episode at a time.** Don't build an ever-widening tree. Each episode is written after the previous one is played,
  deepening the path actually taken. Branch within an episode where it matters, then converge.
- **The pilot opens on move-in day**: Jack's dad's truck, the dorm, meeting Theo, Lena glimpsed once (unnamed) across
  the quad. Then the party, then Mass the next morning.

## Mechanics
- **Hidden relationship meters** per core character: trust, warmth, heat, friction. Never shown as numbers; the player
  feels them in how people talk to Jack.
- **Soul track:** grace vs. drift, honesty vs. performance, restraint vs. abandon. It shapes Jack's inner voice and which
  options are available to him.
- **Jack's traits** firm up through choices (wit, sincerity, charm, candor, recklessness, control) and unlock unique
  dialogue.
- **Journal / clue board**, visible, Strike-style: people, clues, notes, and the story so far.
- **Choice types:** dialogue tone and content; where to go and who to be with (you can't be everywhere); **argument
  duels**, a philosophy-fight mini-game with Lena (pick premises, concede or press; rigor, wit and heart score; winning
  isn't always good for the relationship).
- **Feedback:** Telltale whispers ("Lena will remember that."), used sparingly.
- **Resolve:** hidden resolve for Jack and for Lena, rising and falling with prayer, confession, stress, alcohol and
  loneliness. In charged scenes, the player's choices act on Jack's resolve; Lena's is hidden and set by the writers.
  When both run low, the dangerous night becomes possible.
- **Rewind:** the player may restart the *current* episode from its beginning before finishing it. Once finished, the
  episode is locked forever.
- **Notes session** before every episode (Claude runs it with AskUserQuestion). Honor the notes without spoiling secrets.

## Aesthetic
- **2009 period texture:** prestige-TV base (title cards, "Previously on…", chyron scene headers like
  "THURSDAY · 11:47 PM · DELGADO HALL, 4TH FLOOR") plus period artifacts as gameplay inserts: Facebook wall posts,
  T9 texts on a flip phone, AIM away messages, an iPod.
- **Text needle-drops:** real songs, era-accurate (released by the scene's date), cued at key moments like a TV music
  supervisor would.
- Dark theme by default, with a light toggle.

## Content standards
- **Explicitness:** the player asked for the narrative to be as graphic, explicit and unflinching as permitted, always in
  service of character, story and emotion. In practice: sex, desire, bodies, drug use, violence, addiction, overdose,
  death and grief are written frankly, with real sensory and physical detail, at the level of literary fiction or
  HBO drama. Sex scenes can be on the page and charged, but they are written as literature (emotion, sensation, the
  specific people), not pornographic mechanics. The ensemble's sex lives are shown frankly (Euphoria-honest): the fun,
  the morning after, the loneliness. Neither preachy nor glamorized.
- **Everyone in any sexual content is an adult (18+).** All freshmen are 18 or older; say so in the bible.
- **Sexual assault** may be a storyline, handled seriously and centered on the person's experience and aftermath.
  Never titillating, never a cheap twist.
- Addiction and overdose (someone may not make it), death and grief, crime and violence (a mystery thread) are all allowed.
- **Hard lines:** the narrative never mocks the faith (critics can be sharp and right; the story never sneers). No cheap
  villains or secular strawmen; everyone has their reasons. **Jack and Lena never die.**
