## 1. Calendar and liturgy
- **Sun Nov 1: All Saints**, solemnity, replaces the 31st Sunday OT. Rev 7:2-4, 9-14; Ps 24; 1 John 3:1-3; Matt 5:1-12a (the Beatitudes) [master; E12 sheet]. E13 opens in the afternoon, after the E12 morning Mass: the Delgado kitchen scene is Sunday afternoon (Josie's text). Sunset about 4:58 p.m. PST; the early dark is new this week.
- **Mon Nov 2: ALL SOULS**, the Commemoration of All the Faithful Departed. Not a holy day of obligation. Priests may say three Masses [general canon-law practice]. Vestments violet, white or black (black was still permitted in 2009; many parishes used white or violet). **Lectionary:** a choice of readings; common ones are Wis 3:1-9 ("The souls of the just are in the hand of God, and no torment shall touch them"); Rom 6:3-9 (or Rom 5:5-11); John 6:37-40 ("this is the will of him who sent me, that I should lose nothing of all that he has given me, but raise it up on the last day") [general; the exact choice is the celebrant's; not a lectionary-day lookup]. Wis 3 and John 6:37-40 are the safest.
- **Old translation still in force** (the third edition of the Roman Missal begins Nov 27, 2011) [V in master]: "Eternal rest grant unto them, O Lord, and let perpetual light shine upon them" is the classic prayer; the 1973-ICEL funeral intercession is "Lord, hear our prayer." Responses are "And also with you" / "Thanks be to God."
- **The Book of the Dead / the book of names:** a parish or campus-ministry custom in November: a book (or a list, a basket, a table with candles) in which parishioners write the names of their dead, which are read at Masses through the month or placed near the altar/shrine [general US Catholic custom; the Newman Center's version is the bible's invention]. **Plenary indulgence** for the souls in purgatory: the faithful can gain one by visiting a cemetery and praying for the dead on each day Nov 1-8 (with the usual conditions: confession, Communion, prayers for the Pope's intentions) and, on Nov 2, by visiting a church or oratory and saying the Our Father and the Creed [general; the 2009 Enchiridion Indulgentiarum; a Lena/Osei line, UNVERIFIED in phrasing]. Offering **Mass intentions** for the dead is the normal Catholic practice (Jack's family: Nonno).
- **St. Odilo of Cluny** (abbot, d. 1049) established the Nov 2 commemoration at Cluny in the 990s-1030s and it spread to Rome by the 14th century [general]. **Mémère is named Odile** (bible). This is a free echo: a St. Odilo/St. Odile naming joke or a quiet nod; **St. Odile of Alsace's feast is Dec 13** (patron of the blind) and is a different saint. Do not conflate on the page without the name explained.
- **Tue Nov 3: St. Martin de Porres** (optional memorial; Dominican lay brother of Lima; a mixed-race saint, patron of social justice, hospitals, barbers; canonized 1962). Tuesday of the 31st week OT. **Weekday readings (UNVERIFIED day-assignment):** Rom 12:5-16 and Luke 14:15-24 (the great banquet, "go out to the highways and hedges and compel people to come in, that my house may be filled").
