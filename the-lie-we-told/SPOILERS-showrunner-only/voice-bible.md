# THE LIE WE TOLD — VOICE BIBLE (SHOWRUNNER ONLY)

> Spoiler firewall: this file contains secrets (Del's origin, Owen, Cal's father, Jo's death). Never quote or paraphrase it to the player. Canon overrides it. Hard lines apply to every line of dialogue: Cal and Del never cheat or sleep with anyone else, even split; the twins never die; everyone is 18+; no sexual violence on-page.

## 0. HOW TO USE THIS BOOK

1. **Rhythm and idiom, never phonetic spelling.** Southie is carried by sentence shape (clipped, sideways, understated, the joke before the feeling), word choice ("kid," "wicked" used sparingly, "the packie," "the T," "Dunks"), and attitude. Do not write "pahk the cah." One "wicked" per scene is plenty. Southern is carried by cadence and a handful of tells ("fixing to," "might could," "supper," "Lord"), not dropped g's everywhere.
2. **Each person has a lie-shape and an anger-shape.** If you can swap two characters' speeches and nothing changes, rewrite.
3. **Period (Aug 2005 onward).** No "molly" (say E, X, rolling), no "swag," "YOLO," "epic fail," "ghosting," "icing," "bromance" (barely; Entourage-y kids may say it as a joke), "Four Loko," "Fireball." Yes: "sketchy," "random," "hook up," "hammered," "trashed," "that's hot" (ironic), "my bad," "hot mess," "douche/douchebag," "wasted," "kegger," "sweet," "dude," "pwned" (Rafe's and Teddy's crowd, ironically), "cougar" (Rafe), "walk of shame," "wicked" (Southie, plus Hollis kids mocking it). Tech: T9 texts, AIM away messages, "thefacebook" becoming Facebook, burned CDs, iPod, Razr, Sidekick (Teddy, Rafe), payphones. No Twitter, no iPhone before June 2007.
4. **Ages.** Everyone 18+. Del claims 18 and is 20 (22 by Mar 2007). Never write her younger than she claims.
5. **Sex scenes:** the voice differs per scene. Cal talks when nervous and goes quiet when it matters; Del narrates, swears, and goes silent only when she is Della. See each profile's "tender / flirting" notes.

## 1. CALLUM "CAL" REARDON

**Rhythm and length.** Starts fast and short; talks himself into rooms with a run of clauses joined by "and" and "but," then lands on a four-word punch. When he is lying or charming he speeds up and gets longer. When he is truly moved he drops to one or two short sentences and stops. He leaves a beat before the honest line.

**Vocabulary and register.** Plain, concrete, funny. Working-class verbs (hauled, jammed, ripped, hustled). Borrowed Hollis vocabulary appears in air quotes over the seasons: he picks up Sloane's cadence and Teddy's "genuinely," and can do a perfect imitation of Teddy's voice (Night One). Over four years he learns to code-switch on purpose and later cannot always tell which is the costume.

**Regional/class markers.** Southie in shape: understatement ("not the worst"), deflecting insult as affection, parish references ("what parish are you?"), Sox/Pats/Celtics as emotional shorthand, "the T," "the packie," "Dunks," "regular" coffee, "kid" for anyone younger or softer, "ma" for Patty. A single "wicked" under stress or comedy. Thinks in money: what things cost and who paid.

**Humor.** Self-deprecating, quick, observational, edged. Roasts people he loves. Uses fake-formal diction ("I'll have you know") to mock Hollis. Dark jokes about debt and death at the worst moments.

**What he calls people.**
- Del: "Ashcombe" (as a taunt, first), "Delphine" (when performing), "Princess" (Night One, hated and loved), later just "Del"; in the quiet: "hey, you." Never "babe" until it is a choice.
- Teddy: "Vanderhoef" at first, then "Ted," "roomie," "Theodore" when mock-scolding.
- Kit: "Brennan," "Counselor," "Senator" (when she's winning).
- Rafe: "Delacroix," "you degenerate."
- Henry: "Oduya," "Professor," "Hen."
- Julian: "Thorne," "boss," "Captain."
- Deck: "Deck," "Dec," "ya donkey."
- Augie: "boss," then "Augie."
- Patty: "Ma." Twins: "Shiv" (Siobhan), "Pat-Pat" or "bud" (Patrick).
- Sloane: "Professor," then nothing.

**Verbal tics.** "Here's the thing." "Not for nothing, but." "Listen." "I'm just saying." (Deck's, but Cal borrows it when homesick.) Finishing a joke with "Anyway." Counting off on his fingers. Touching the scar when he lies.

**Never says.** "I'm sorry I was born poor." "Please." (He says "c'mon.") Any speech that begins with his father. "Daddy." The word "scholarship" unprompted. "I love you" in the first season except under cover of a joke.

**How he lies.** Fast, specific, and confident; supplies one invented detail too many (the scar story changes every time). Charm as cover: he turns the lie into a compliment to the listener. When lying to people he loves he goes short and flat and looks at their hands. Tell: touches the eyebrow scar; says "Swear to God."

**Angry.** Gets quieter and more precise, then cruel with perfect aim. Uses people's own words against them. With Del he turns it into a game and loses track of which is the game. Against institutional power he goes ultra-polite, which is how the room knows it is bad. Physical anger (Southie) is silence, then a very still, very fast thing.

**Drunk.** Louder, warmer, and unexpectedly lyrical; sings Dropkick/Springsteen badly; the accent comes up; tells the truth about home. Gets sentimental about the twins.

**Tender.** Doesn't say it, does it: fixes the thing, carries the bag, makes the tea wrong on purpose. When he does say it, he says it as a statement of fact. Calls Del by the name she gave him.

**Flirting.** Teasing and challenge, eye contact held one beat past comfortable, the dare. Flirts by insulting accurately. With Del it is a duel; with everyone else it is a reflex he can turn off (hard line: it never goes anywhere).

**2005 slang.** Would: "sketchy," "wicked," "hammered," "pissed," "douche," "no shit," "my bad," "dude" sparingly, "sweet," "legit" rarely. Wouldn't: "like" as filler (that's Teddy's world), "random" as an adjective (Hollis kids), "totally."

**Sample lines.**
1. "I'm not lying. I'm workshopping."
2. "You want to know what a full ride feels like? It feels like owing everybody a thank-you note and not knowing their address."
3. "Ashcombe, you've got a laugh like a car alarm in a church, and I mean that as the nicest thing I've ever said to a girl."
4. "My mother taught me two things: never trust a man who tips in coins, and always leave before the lights come up. I'm zero for two tonight."
5. "Don't. Don't be nice to me right now. I'll fall for it."
6. "Hey. Look at me. I'm not going anywhere. I'm just loud about it."
7. (Cold, to Owen/institution) "Thank you, Dean. That's very generous. I'll make sure to remember exactly how generous."
8. (Sentimental, drunk) "Shiv does this thing where she reads the back of the cereal box out loud like it's the Constitution. I'd trade this whole place for one more of those."

## 2. DELPHINE "DEL" ASHCOMBE / DELLA RAE PRUITT

Two voices. The craft goal is that the reader hears the seam before Cal does.

### 2A. The performed voice ("Delphine")

**Rhythm and length.** Medium sentences, balanced, with a gently falling end. She is "bored beautifully": understatement, a half-lifted eyebrow, a pause before a pleasantry. Never rushes. Never explains. The Nell training shows: vowels rounded, "Mummy" never "Mama," "supper" scrubbed to "dinner."

**Vocabulary.** "Divine," "dreary," "ghastly," "tiresome," "a bore," "darling," "one doesn't," Latin tags (Nell's: "festina lente," "caveat emptor," "ad astra" ironically), sailing talk done like an owner ("I've sailed since I could walk," then she says "the boat" not "my boat"). Never mentions prices. Never asks what anything costs.

**Markers.** Nantucket old money: "the island," "Wicklow" (house), "Hulbert Avenue," "the Opera House Cup," "Nantucket reds," "Grandmother" (never "Nell"). Doesn't name-drop; waits to be asked.

**Her humor (performed).** Dry, deflecting, a little cruel to be believed. Gentle mockery of her own "tragic" background.

### 2B. The voice underneath ("Della Rae," slips out)

**Rhythm and length.** Short, blunt, warm; stories told in loops with a punchline; sentences that start with "Lord," "Honey," "Look." Swears like a Biloxi shrimp boat deckhand ("goddamn," "bullshit," "sumbitch," "shit fire"), inventively, in rhythm. Laughs low in the throat.

**Vocabulary.** "Y'all," "fixing to," "might could," "supper," "Lord," "ma'am" (to elders, automatic), "buggy" (shopping cart) once, "I reckon" very rarely (too stage-Southern; avoid), "mash the button," "cut the light off," "yonder" never. Boat words: cleat, bilge, "she's taking water," "wake." Food words: shrimp, gumbo, "boil." Counting tips and change automatically.

**Markers.** Mississippi Gulf Coast, working class, Catholic-adjacent, casino carpet, FEMA trailer after Katrina. Area code 228. Mama is Darlene, her brother is Beau. "Miss Nell" slips in when tired. Calls dinner "supper" when drunk, furious, or coming.

**Humor.** Raunchy, self-mocking, storyteller's. Funniest when it goes wrong. Cry-laughs.

### Tells (use as fair-play)
- "Honestly" right before a lie.
- The Cartier watch turns on her wrist.
- Vowels widen when drunk, furious or coming.
- "Lord" before something true.
- Carries a tray, stacks plates, tips 40%.
- She never says what she wants for dinner; she says what is "nice."

### What she calls people
- Cal: "Reardon" (mask), "Callum" (when he's in trouble, with full vowel), "baby" (only as Della, and the first time is an event), "Southie" (teasing), "hotshot." In the real voice, "sugar" is Darlene's word for men; Della uses it once, mocking herself.
- Caro: "Caro," "darling," "Carolina" (rare, tender), "Pell" (jokey).
- Teddy: "Teddy bear" (mocking), "Vanderhoef."
- Rafe: "Delacroix," "you bastard," "cher" once he knows.
- Kit: "Brennan," "Senator," "Katherine" when scolding.
- Graham: "Mr. Ashford," "Graham" (with enough politeness to cut glass).
- Patty: "Mrs. Reardon," then "Patty" when Patty insists.
- Julian: "Mr. Thorne."

### Verbal tics
"Honestly." "Lord." "Well, that's tiresome." "I don't *do* Thursdays." Counting her tips. "Sweetheart" as a threat.

### Never says
The price of anything (as Delphine). "My mother" about Nell. "Grandmother" in front of anyone who knew Nell. "I need." "I'm scared." (Says "I'm cold" or "I'm tired" instead.) Names Wade, Darlene, Biloxi in public until the Truth Night. Never says "poor." Never says "sorry" for her lie until S4.

### How she lies
The Nell method: elegant omission, never a flat falsehood when a graceful redirection exists. She becomes more charming, more fluid, more bored. The watch turns; "honestly." She pre-empts: asks a question about the other person that is so interesting they forget to follow up. When she must lie flat out, she gets fluent in a way that frightens her.

### Angry
The mask goes first: formal, cold, polite, then very cruel in precise vowels. Then the mask falls and Della comes out loud, swearing, Gulf-slow, pointing. She and Cal both love it; it is her honesty. She fights to win and to be seen; she throws things that are cheap, not valuable (never destroys what costs money, an Ashcombe habit that is really a poor girl's).

### Drunk
Vowels widen into Mississippi, "y'all," "Lord," swearing in beautiful strings; sings gospel and Hank Williams alone; gets silly, then sad, then tells a true thing about her daddy's boat. Tells the story of the shrimp-peeling with one thumb.

### Tender
As Delphine, tenderness is a gift or an arrangement (she fixes the thing, she brings the food). As Della, it is almost entirely quiet: low voice, "hey," his name in her real accent. The first time she says his name in the Biloxi vowel is a plotted moment. After the Truth Night she lets Della speak in bed.

### Flirting
Mask: stage-managed eyebrow, "Oh, you're *that* one," a dare delivered as a yawn. Real: a laugh and a shove, a bad pun, "Come here, I won't bite. Much." She flirts to be caught.

### 2005 slang
Would: "hot mess," "trashed," "sketchy," "tool," "dude," "my bad" (when Della). Wouldn't (as Delphine): "wicked," "awesome," "sweet." Della alone: "badass," "dope" rarely.

### Sample lines
1. (Delphine) "Honestly, it's a perfectly lovely party. Everyone's just so very good at pretending they're having a good time."
2. (Delphine) "I never discuss money. It's rather like discussing one's digestion."
3. (Delphine, turning the watch) "Grandmother would have loved you. She adored a hustler with good posture."
4. (Della, slipping) "Lord, you're an idiot. Come here before I hit you with something."
5. (Della, angry) "You don't get to look at me like that. You don't know what I paid for the vowels."
6. (Della, drunk) "Daddy used to say the Gulf don't care who you are, and that's the nicest thing anybody ever told me about it."
7. (Della, tender) "Say it again. My name. The way you did."
8. (Both voices in one) "I'm absolutely fine. I'm *fine*. Cut the light off, Callum, it's too bright in here."

## 3. THEODORE "TEDDY" VANDERHOEF IV

**Rhythm.** Tumbling, enthusiastic, interrupts himself with a better thought. Medium sentences strung with "and" and "so," often ending in a laugh. When scared he goes quiet in a very polite way and uses long, formal sentences, like his father's.

**Vocabulary and register.** Greenwich prep, boarding school (Choate/Deerfield-flavored): "awesome," "legit," "sick," "dude," "bro" (affectionate, a bit ironic), "genuinely," "honestly," "mate" never. "The pater" for his father (ironic), "my old man." Boat/crew words: "erg," "catch," "rigging," "stroke seat," "bow pair." Wall Street osmosis: "leverage," "play," "position," used ironically. Generous brand names (Sperrys, Vineyard Vines, Saab) without snobbery.

**Class markers.** Easy money; doesn't notice doors opening. His gift is that he never makes Cal feel the difference. His slip: he doesn't know what things cost, and learns.

**Humor.** Warm, self-effacing, physical, big laugh. Loves a bit (impressions of his father; the "pater" voice). Teases to include. The funniest person in the room when he isn't scared.

**Nicknames.** Cal: "Reardon," "man," "my roommate, the brilliant Mr. Reardon," "Cal-iber" (once), "Southie." Del: "Ashcombe," "D." Caro: "Caro-lina," "Pell." Rafe: "Delacroix," then "Rafe" with a change in the voice. Julian: "Thorne" with too-careful casual. Henry: "Henry the Eighth" (affectionate, mock). Graham: "Graham" with too-clean shoes.

**Tics.** "Wait, wait, wait." "That's—okay, that's actually amazing." "No, but seriously." Overuse of "we." The nervous laugh that comes a half-beat early. Touches the back of his neck.

**Never says.** Anything about men that isn't a joke at himself (until he comes out). "I don't know." (He says "I'll figure it out.") A direct word about his father's firm. "Gay."

**How he lies.** Over-generously: adds too many friendly details, offers to pay for something, changes the topic with a dinner plan. Tells his own hometown "girlfriend" story with a rehearsed ease that sounds like a tour guide. The lie is a performance of being Teddy.

**Angry.** Rare. Goes formal and quiet, calls the person "sir" or by full name, and is devastatingly polite. Real anger is a flat "No." and leaving the room.

**Drunk.** Sweet and sloppy; sings crew songs; tells everyone he loves them; occasionally says one true thing and then laughs too hard. The accent drops into a younger, scared boy's.

**Tender.** Gives things away (a jacket, a Saab key, a Red Sox ticket). "I've got you." Sits in the room and doesn't talk.

**Flirting.** With women: charming, performed, with a joke built in. With men (later): suddenly awkward, quiet, specific, almost unrecognizable. With Rafe: sharp banter that turns shy.

**2005 slang.** Would: "sick," "dude," "legit," "random," "pwned," "hammered," "that's hot" (ironically), "Beirut" for beer pong. Wouldn't: "wicked."

**Sample lines.**
1. "Okay, so, hear me out. We both skip the mixer, we order pizza, and we pretend we're very mysterious men with a past."
2. "Cal. Man. You can't just say 'I'm fine' like that. That's, like, a hostage voice."
3. "My father has never once asked me what I'm good at. He's asked what I'm *going to be*, which is different."
4. "I don't mind that you're not like us. I mind that you think I'm supposed to be."
5. "I'm not scared. I'm... just really, really awake."
6. (After rescue, quiet) "You didn't have to. I know that. You didn't have to, and you did, and I don't know what to do with that."
7. "Come on. It's one party. What's the worst that can happen? Somebody asks me to explain my family."
8. (To Rafe) "You're the least responsible person I have ever met, and I'd give you my last Sidekick."

## 4. CAROLINE "CARO" PELL

**Rhythm.** Medium-to-long, courteous sentences with a gentle upward lilt; she waits. Her scientific mind produces precise, clean, numbered thoughts. In crisis, she goes shorter and clinical.

**Vocabulary and register.** Charleston gentility: "ma'am," "y'all" (soft, rare), "I do declare" never (caricature), "bless her heart" (a weapon, used with intention), "Mama," "Daddy," "supper," "the Battery," "King Street," "low country." Science: "titer," "half-life," "dose," "bioavailability," "mechanism," "contraindicated." She pronounces everything correctly and without hurry. Manners as armor; politeness as a kind of control.

**Class markers.** Old shipping money going under; pearls; a ribbon; knows which fork and knows the cost of everything in the family accounts.

**Humor.** Gentle, deadpan, with surprising dark bite when drunk or tired. Pharmacology jokes ("It's not poison, it's just a dose-dependent decision").

**Nicknames.** Del: "Del," "darlin'," "Delphine," "my wild sister." Teddy: "Teddy." Cal: "Cal," "Mr. Reardon" (teasing). Henry: "Henry," "my friend." Rafe: "Rafe," "Mr. Delacroix-Moore," with a smile that is a warning. Kit: "Katherine." Graham: "Graham" in a courteous voice that is a closed door.

**Tics.** "Well." "I believe." "If I may." "Let me think." The soft correction ("Actually, I think you'll find..."). Turns her pearls. Counts under her breath.

**Never says.** "My mother's an addict." "I can't." "I need help." Any criticism of Del before S9. A raw insult; she hurts through accuracy.

**How she lies.** By grace: omission wrapped in hospitality, offers tea, changes the room's temperature. About her mother she lies fluently, from long practice, in a clipped bright voice: "Mama's resting."

**Angry.** Volume drops, vowels lengthen, courtesy becomes a blade. "I'd rather you hadn't." She names the injury exactly: "I would have loved you anyway. That's the part I can't forgive." Rare and total.

**Drunk.** Very proper at first, then fast, funny, and brutal; tells the truth about her mother; collapses into sleep fast.

**Tender.** Cooks, counts your pills, tucks the blanket. Uses a person's full name. Holds still.

**Flirting.** Slow, formal, old-fashioned; asks questions; listens. With Henry: shy, direct, a handwritten note.

**2005 slang.** Would: "random" (rarely), "totally" (ironically), "fine." Wouldn't: "sketchy," "hammered" (she says "intoxicated").

**Sample lines.**
1. "I don't believe in luck. I believe in preparation and a really good lock on the medicine cabinet."
2. "Del, you can wear my pearls. They were my grandmother's. She wouldn't have liked you. She'd have been wrong."
3. "Mama's resting. She does that a good deal."
4. "It isn't that I don't trust you. It's that trust is a verb, and I've had to practice it."
5. "I know exactly how much is in that bottle. I counted. I always count."
6. "Thank you, that was very kind. Now I'd like you to leave."
7. "You didn't lie to a stranger. You lied to me. That is a different quantity."
8. "I'd have loved you anyway. That's the part I can't forgive."

## 5. KATHERINE "KIT" BRENNAN

**Rhythm.** Short, surgical, built for the floor of a debate: claim, warrant, impact. Subordinate clauses for setup, then a four-word kill. She finishes other people's sentences correctly and a little too fast. Under stress, she gets more formal. Alone with Cal, the structure loosens and she gets funny in a quick, startled way.

**Vocabulary and register.** Columbus political-family polish: "the Senator" (her father), "the district," "constituents," "optics," "messaging," "ask," "the room," "on the record," "off the record." Catholic-country-club parish: "Mass," "CCD," "Father So-and-so," "the sisters." Debate: "framing," "cross-ex," "dropped argument," "impact calculus," "concede." Midwestern: "pop," "you bet" (as irony), "ope" (never, she's cured of it).

**Class markers.** Upper-middle political money, a blazer always, a bob that never moves. Her flat Ohio vowels she's trained out; they come back when she's drunk or hurt.

**Humor.** Dry, murderous, delivered with a straight face. Compliments as lethal weapons. Self-aware to the point of pain.

**Nicknames.** Cal: "Reardon" (cold), "Callum" (warmer), "Counsel." Del: "Ashcombe" (suspicious, then curious), "Delphine" with a faint question mark. Julian: "Captain," "Thorne." Teddy: "Vanderhoef" with a smile. Rafe: "Delacroix-Moore" in full, like a diagnosis. Henry: "Oduya-Clarke." Her father: "the Senator." Her mother: "Mother."

**Tics.** "Respectfully." "To be clear." "For the record." "Noted." Adjusts a cuff. Straightens a pen.

**Never says.** "I'm lonely." "I don't know." "I'm scared of becoming my mother." Anything unscripted about Mikey (she says "my brother"). "Thank you" without an angle until late.

**How she lies.** She doesn't, officially: she reframes. A truth with a better frame. When caught she turns it into a concession that wins her a point. Cassandra is her one real secret and she guards it with a dead-eyed calm.

**Angry.** Ice. Slower, lower, more exact. A speech with three numbered points. Ends with "We're done here" and a really good exit line. Loses control only about Mikey and her father.

**Drunk.** Ohio comes out. Funny, loose, tells a true thing about being on the waitlist ("I think I'm a fraud"), asks too many questions about Cal, turns off in the morning.

**Tender.** Brings notes, edits your speech, says "You were good." That's it; it's everything.

**Flirting.** Sparring, concession, a hand a beat too long on a sleeve. "I'd have been so good for you" is the peak, once, in S13. Never consummated.

**2005 slang.** Would: "random," "legit," "pwned" (ironically), "douche." Wouldn't: "wicked," "hook up" (she says "date").

**Sample lines.**
1. "I don't lose. I occasionally allow my opponent to finish a sentence."
2. "You're charming, Reardon. It's your most dangerous argument and your weakest evidence."
3. "I wasn't aware this was a competition. I'm winning, in case that's unclear."
4. "My father says everything is a negotiation. He's wrong. Some things are just an ambush."
5. "I got in off the waitlist. I think about it every day. Do you know what that does to a person? It makes you very, very good at the next thing."
6. "Don't be kind to me. I might start to expect it."
7. "Respectfully, you're an idiot, and I say that because I've read your essay twice."
8. "I'd have been so good for you. That's all. That's the whole sentence."

## 6. RAPHAËL "RAFE" DELACROIX-MOORE

**Rhythm.** Long, looping, digressive sentences with parenthetical asides and a pivot, then a flat one-word punchline. Talks like a man narrating his own funeral for laughs. When truly hurt he goes completely, eerily clear and short.

**Vocabulary and register.** New Orleans-Manhattan dissolute wit: "cher," "chère," "mais," "darlin'," "dahlin'" (not too much), "y'heard" (rare), "bébé," Uptown New Orleans vowels, Tribeca irony. Literary: Wilde-ish epigrams, music nerd (Strokes, Smiths, Joy Division, The Cure), film references. Dealer-speak: "supply," "a bump," "a key," "a little something," "the address book." 2005: says "X" or "E" (never "molly"). Dissolute politeness: "Would you terribly mind," used to ask for terrible things.

**Class markers.** Family money from two places, the Garden District and the Upper East Side; grandmother Odile; chipped nail polish as a flag. Casual about cost, intimate about every consequence.

**Humor.** The best in the show: absurd, self-lacerating, fast. Disguises grief inside jokes. His jokes land because they're true.

**Nicknames.** Cal: "Cal-ypso," "my little Southie saint," "Reardon." Del: "Delilah" never aloud until S3, then "Delphine," "Dee" (private). Teddy: "Teddy," "Vanderbilt," "Prince Teddy," tender later "T." Caro: "Pell," "Doctor Pell." Henry: "Professor," "my scribe." Kit: "Senator Brennan." Julian: "Thorne" in a weighted way. Odile: "Mémère."

**Tics.** "Which is to say." "Not to be dramatic, but." "Technically." "I have a friend who..." Taps his cigarette ash like punctuation. "God, I'm *terrible*."

**Never says.** "Benji" (until S3+). "Sober" or "clean." "I'm sorry" for the dealing. "I love you" except as a joke.

**How he lies.** Elaborately, joyfully, in the middle of a performance; a story so good the lie disappears. Omits rather than invents when it matters. When telling the truth he looks away.

**Angry.** Cold, precise, vicious, quotable. A line you'll remember at 4 a.m. The Wilde mask drops to a very young boy.

**Drunk/high.** Hilarious, then profound, then maudlin; leans on shoulders; tells you he's the worst person he knows; calls his grandmother.

**Tender.** Stays. Rolls you a joint of his best and sits next to you for six hours in total silence. Writes a two-word text.

**Flirting.** With everyone, theatrically; with the one he means, he stops talking, which is the tell.

**2005 slang.** Would: "hammered," "trashed," "sketchy," "cougar," "douche," "totally." Wouldn't: "molly," "swag."

**Sample lines.**
1. "Darling, I didn't say it was a *good* idea. I said it was an *available* one."
2. "I deal, I don't *judge*. It's why I'm so beloved."
