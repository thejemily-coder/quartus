Hard lines are absolute: Cal and Del never cheat and never sleep with anyone else, even while split; Siobhan and Patrick never die; Cal and Del sleep together on Night One; every character is 18+ from the first scene (Del is OLDER than she claims, never younger); sexual violence is never depicted on-page or eroticized. **This bible contains none.**

## 1. LOGLINE & THEMATIC SPINE

**Logline.** On the first night of college, a Southie scholarship kid impersonating a legacy and a girl working the party she's crashing catch each other mid-con, swear a pact of silence — "we were never here" — and fall into bed and into love. Then it turns out "here" was the riverbank where a townie girl died two weeks earlier, and the person who covered it up saw them both.

**The title.** *The lie we told* is literally the Night One pact. At 1:50 a.m. on Aug 28, 2005, Cal and Del, hiding at the Ladder dam shrine, watch a figure in a Corinthian blazer take a painted oar blade off Jo Lavoie's memorial and throw it into the mill race. Both are mid-con (Cal impersonating Teddy Vanderhoef at a club he'll be thrown out of the college for crashing; Del working the party as staff under a borrowed apron). They agree: *We were never here — not at the party, not at the river.* That lie protects them. It also protects the man who killed Jo, for three and a half years. The college-era finale is the night they finally take it back, together, at a cost to both.

**What the show is about underneath.**
1. **The lies we tell to become someone.** Del built a whole person. Cal impersonates a legacy on Night One and spends four years doing it in subtler ways. Julian Thorne built "Julian Thorne." Owen Tierney built "Owen Tierney, Hollis man." Hollis itself is a lie told for 214 years about merit. The question is never "is lying bad" — it's *which lies make you more yourself and which ones eat you.*
2. **Class.** Who serves and who is served. The show keeps putting people on the wrong side of the service door: Del in an apron; Cal behind the bar; Jo, who died because she was invited to the wrong side of the rope for one night.
3. **Inheritance.** Money, names, secrets, fathers, guilt. Cal inherits a father and a fortune he didn't ask for; Del inherits a dead (later: dying) girl's name; Teddy inherits his father's 1979 sin; Henry inherits his father's silence.
4. **Who gets to be careless.** The rich get to be careless because someone cleans up. The tragedy of the antagonist is that he is a poor boy who learned the rich rule — and the institution cleaned up for him because by then he was theirs. Cal and Del are "two arsonists": the series asks whether they can be reckless without being careless. (Recklessness risks yourself. Carelessness spends other people.)

**Motifs.** The river (Lethe = forgetting); service doors; names on things (boathouses, prizes, apron tapes); 20-minute delays (Cal's July 4 and Lethe's Aug 13 are the same sin at different prices); photographs (Del's Polaroids vs. Jo's waterlogged disposable vs. the antagonist's digital prints); coins (the Lethe obol; the pay-phone quarter; Del's saved sugar packets).

## 2. DEL'S TRUTH

### Identity
- **Real name: Della Rae Pruitt.**
- **Real DOB: March 3, 1985.** At move-in (Aug 27, 2005) she is **20 years, 5 months.** She claims to be 18 (Delphine's DOB, Nov 11, 1986). She is 22 on March 3, 2007; Cal learns her true age then (S8).
- **Born:** Biloxi, Mississippi — Point Cadet, the old seafood-processing neighborhood on the east end. Area code **228**.
- **Father:** Wade Pruitt, shrimper (boat: the *Miss Darlene*), drowned in a squall off Horn Island, Sept 1996, when Della was 11. Body recovered. She learned to swim like a fish out of spite.
- **Mother:** Darlene Pruitt (née Ladner), b. 1964, 41 in 2005. Cocktail waitress in the Biloxi casinos after Wade died; pills (Lortab) and bad men. Loving, useless, funny. Calls Della "Della Rae" when sober, "baby" when not.
- **Half-brother:** Beau Pruitt, b. 1990 (15 in 2005), from Darlene's brief second marriage. Della half-raised him — her twins. She has sent money to him since she was 16.

### The road to "Delphine Ashcombe"
- **2001:** leaves Biloxi High at 16 to work (diner, then the Back Bay supper club). GED as Della Pruitt, 2002.
- **March 2003 (age 18):** dancer/waitress in the revue at the **Starlite Lounge**, a Back Bay Biloxi supper club owned by the uncle of **Travis Lacombe** (b. 1971), a casino host who ran pills and loans on the side. Her stage name on the flyer: **"Delilah Rae."** It was a supper-club revue, feathers and kick lines — not sex work. (Room rule: keep it that way.)
- **May 2003:** Travis broke Darlene's wrist over a debt. Della, 18, took **$23,000** in cash from the safe in Travis's office (keys copied from his jacket while she was waiting tables) — partly his loan money, partly what he'd taken from Darlene and others. She left $3,000 with Darlene and Beau and got on a Greyhound north. There is an open **Harrison County felony larceny warrant for Della Rae Pruitt** (Travis reported it as a staff theft without naming the amount; he wanted her found, not the money examined).
- **June 2003:** Nantucket. Answered a "summer island help" board at a Hyannis hostel. Housekeeping on the island. Cleaned **Wicklow**, the shingled Hulbert Avenue house of **Eleanor "Nell" Ashcombe** (b. 1922; widow of **Bertram "Bertie" Ashcombe**, Hollis '49, Lethe '49; d. 1991).
- **Winter 2003–04:** Nell, 81, sharp, lonely, mischievous, asked Della to stay on as live-in companion. Della did. Nell taught her everything: vowels, forks, sailing, Latin tags, how to sit, how to be bored beautifully, how old money never says the price of anything. Della taught Nell gin rummy and how to swear in Cajun French.
- **The real Delphine.** Nell's granddaughter **Delphine Eleanor Ashcombe**, b. Nov 11, 1986, daughter of Nell's son **Peter Ashcombe** (drunk; d. 2000, car, Polpis Road). In July 1997, age 10, Delphine nearly drowned when Peter capsized their Beetle Cat off Great Point. **Anoxic brain injury.** Since 1998 she has lived, nonverbal and dependent, at **Clinique Les Tilleuls, Montreux, Switzerland.** Nell — out of old-money shame and fierce love — told Nantucket society for eight years that "Delphine is at school in Switzerland." *That is why the con works:* society expects a Delphine to come home from Swiss schooling. Almost no one has seen her since she was ten.
- **Nov 2004:** Nell took Della to Montreux (Della got a real passport as Della Pruitt for the trip — it's in the lining of her trunk). Della sat with Delphine for three days and read to her. This is Della's secret grief and her deepest guilt: she is living the life of a girl who is alive and cannot live it.
- **The plan (Nell's, June–Oct 2004).** Nell decided Della would "go to Bertie's college" as Delphine. Nell held Delphine's birth certificate (Nantucket County) and Social Security card as guardian. Paperwork:
  - **Massachusetts driver's license** as Delphine (Nell drove her to the RMV in Hyannis with the birth certificate; 2004 rules).
  - **Swiss transcripts** from **École Hirondelle, Montreux**, a small finishing-school-cum-lycée that closed in June 2004 after its director's bankruptcy. Records "archived" with the former director, **Madame Odile Ferrand**, who produced transcripts for "Delphine" for 40,000 CHF from Nell. Ferrand is alive in Vevey and bribable — and blackmail-capable (later pressure point).
  - **SAT** taken as Delphine at a Boston test center, Oct 2004: **1490**. Del is genuinely brilliant. Nothing about her mind is fake.
  - **Hollis Early Decision**, Nov 1, 2004. Essay: sailing, and "my father, who drowned" — true for both girls. **Recommendation letter from Nell to Dean Philip Carrow** (Bertie was Carrow's Lethe "elder"). Admitted Dec 15, 2004.
- **Nell dies Jan 22, 2005.** Her will (public in Nantucket Probate Court) leaves **"my companion, Della Pruitt," $25,000** — a public, discoverable record. Off the books, Nell had given Della the key to a Boston safe deposit box: **$84,000 cash, Nell's pearls, her 1940s Cartier Tank watch, Nell's couture (altered), a monogrammed "E.A." Vuitton steamer trunk**, and a **sealed envelope: "For Della — open it when you're ready to stop."** She has never opened it.
- **Feb 2005:** Nell's nephew **Lowell Ashcombe** (b. 1959, Hollis '81, Lethe '81, Boston estate lawyer, executor) evicted "the companion" from Wicklow and sold it. **Lowell has met Della Pruitt face-to-face, several times.** He does not know about the Hollis plan.
- **Summer 2005:** Della lived in a rented room in Edgartown (Martha's Vineyard), drove the launch at a yacht club as "Dee" (the tan), and practiced being Delphine for eleven weeks.

### Money (a ticking clock)
- Started with $84,000 + $25,000 bequest = $109,000. Hollis 2005–06 all-in ~ $44,000, paid by cashier's check (the Bursar thinks it's eccentric old money).
- Cover story: "Grandmother's estate is in probate litigation; my trust doesn't release until I'm 21; the cards are frozen." Explains the cash.
- After Katrina she sends Darlene and Beau money constantly (they're in a FEMA trailer in Gulfport). **She is broke by the end of sophomore fall (Nov 2006).** This forces S7: Del working at the Race, and S11–S12: Owen's money.

### What she's running from
1. **Travis Lacombe** and the Harrison County warrant (Della Pruitt is a wanted woman).
2. **Federal exposure:** using a living person's identity and SSN is aggravated identity theft. She doesn't fully know how serious this is until Cal (pre-law) does in S5.
3. **Lowell Ashcombe,** who would recognize her instantly.
4. **Being Della.** The real fear: that Della is nobody.

### Who/what could expose her (pressure points, in rough order)
- **Graham Ashford** — saw her serve champagne at his mother's Opera House Cup party at the Ashford house, Hulbert Avenue, Aug 2004. Recognizes her S1 finale.
- **Rafe** — saw "Delilah Rae" dance at the Starlite in summer 2003 (he was 17, on a cousins' trip to the casinos). "I know you from somewhere" from the pilot on. Remembers S3 finale.
- **Annabelle Pell** (Caro's mother) — knew Peter Ashcombe; remembers "that poor little girl sent to Switzerland." Asks questions with a Charleston smile.
- **Maria Fonseca** — Cape Verdean-Nantucket housekeeper who worked beside Della at Wicklow. Calls out "Della?" on Nantucket in the S1 finale.
- **Patty Reardon** — clocks her as ex-waitstaff at Thanksgiving (S2). Patty knows a working girl's hands.
- **Dean Carrow** — sits on Nell's letter, which says: *"She is not what her papers say, Philip. She is better than all of you. Bertie owes her this, and you owe Bertie."* He chose not to look.
- **The public probate record** naming "companion Della Pruitt."
- **Facebook** — News Feed (Sept 5, 2006) and open registration (Sept 26, 2006) make every tagged photo of "Delphine Ashcombe" findable by anyone. Del's no-photos rule becomes a war with her own friends.
- **Lowell** at Homecoming 2006 (S6).
- **The real Delphine's death** — Jan 19, 2008, Montreux. A death record now exists for the identity Del is wearing. Owen uses it (S15).

### Why Hollis specifically
1. **Practical:** Bertie's name and Carrow's debt made admission possible; a Swiss transcript reads fine to a dean who wants it to.
2. **Nell's wish:** "Bertie's college." She made Della promise.
3. **The real reason (sealed, Del doesn't know until she opens the letter in S13):** Bertie Ashcombe was at the tiller of the Lethe launch during the society's Crossing in August **1948**, when a sixteen-year-old townie boy, **Arthur Bouchard**, a dock hand at the Landing, was knocked overboard and drowned. Lethe buried it. Bertie confessed it to Nell on his deathbed. Nell's letter asks Della to "do what Bertie never had the courage to do" and gives her Bertie's 1948 Lethe pocket diary (in the safe deposit box, wrapped in the pearls). Nell sent a con artist to Hollis to burn down the society that made her husband a coward. Del's arc — finding out what she wants — ends with **she wants the truth told, including her own.**

### Ashcombe at Hollis (real connections)
- **The Ashcombe Prize for Rhetoric** — endowed 1962 by Bertie; the Disputation Society's freshman/sophomore prize. Awarded each December; by tradition presented by an Ashcombe if one is on campus. Dean Carrow asks "Delphine" to present it (S2). Cal and Kit compete for it.
- **Lowell Ashcombe '81,** a Lethe **Warden** (alumni trustee of the society) and Hollis Annual Fund chair.
- **Dean Carrow** — Bertie's Lethe protégé.
- **Annabelle Pell** knew Peter.
- **Graham's family** summers four houses down Hulbert Avenue from Wicklow.

### Del's tells (fair-play; writers should use them)
- Turns Nell's Cartier watch on her wrist when lying.
- Says "honestly" right before a lie.
- Southern vowels leak when drunk, furious or coming: "y'all," "fixing to," "Lord," "might could." Calls dinner "supper."
- Carries four plates / a tray one-handed without thinking; stacks dishes for busboys; tips 40%; can't walk past a spill.
- Peels shrimp with one thumb. Can gut a fish. Knows boats like a worker, not a yachtsman ("cleat," "bilge," "she's taking water").
- Saves sugar packets and hotel soap.
- Flinches at the smell of menthol cigarettes and casino carpet.
- Calls Nell "Miss Nell" if she's not careful (a granddaughter would never).
- Says "'85 was a good year" (pilot, about the Krug).
- Her apartment-level competence: fixes a running toilet, rewires a lamp, knows how to get blood out of cotton (cold water, salt).
- Doesn't own a single photograph of herself before 2004. Avoids cameras — but carries a disposable one and photographs everything else.

## 3. CAL'S TRUTHS

### A. The summer of 2005 (Deck is the only one who knows — and even Deck doesn't know Patrick saw)
- **Ricky Dolan** (Patrick "Ricky" Dolan, b. 1964), Patty's on-and-off boyfriend since 2003; collector for **Mickey Coughlin's** crew (loan-sharking and trucks out of a D Street social club).
- **July 4, 2005, ~11:40 p.m.** Patty working a double at **the Quiet Man** on West Broadway. Ricky came to the Reardons' third-floor apartment (triple-decker, **27 Gold Street**, Southie) drunk, looking for Patty's tip coffee can. Patrick, 12, tried to stop him. Ricky backhanded him across the kitchen.
- Cal came home from the fireworks at Castle Island. Fight on the rear porch landing. Ricky's class ring split Cal's eyebrow (**the scar is seven weeks old in the pilot — still pink. Del notices.**). Cal shoved him. Ricky went backward down the back stairs, two flights, onto the concrete of the yard.
- Cal called **Deck**, not 911. Deck (two blocks away) came over the back fences in four minutes. Deck checked Ricky and said "He's gone, Cal. They'll put it on you. Coughlin will put it on you." They spent **twenty minutes** making it a drunk's fall (rinsed the blood off Cal, put Ricky's bottle by his hand, took Ricky's wallet with the $1,100 he'd stolen from Patty earlier that week). Deck called 911 from the payphone at the L Street Store as "a neighbor heard a crash."
- **Ricky was not dead.** He died at Boston Medical Center **July 8, 2005**, without regaining consciousness. Ruled accidental fall, BAC 0.24. Patty "found him" when she came home at 2:15 a.m. to cops and believes it was an accident.
- **Deck's buried truth (S14/S17 reveal):** When Deck knelt by Ricky, Ricky was breathing. Deck said "he's gone" anyway — to protect Cal, and because Ricky once broke Deck's uncle's hand. *The twenty minutes was Deck's choice.* Deck has let Cal carry it. When it comes out: "You didn't kill him, Cal. If anybody did, it was me."
- **Patrick saw.** From the twins' back bedroom window he watched Cal and Deck crouch over Ricky and not call anyone. He has told no one, not even Siobhan. He reveals it to Cal at the end of S5 ("I saw you and Deck. On the stairs. I didn't tell. I won't."). Patrick's silence is a 12-year-old's loyalty and a wound.
- **Consequences that ride:** Coughlin suspects. Deck "squared it" with Coughlin by taking on jobs (driving, carrying) — that's his debt and why he is deeper in the crew than Cal knows. Coughlin is also, by S3, seeing Patty (she doesn't know he suspects Cal).
- **Who else learns:** Cordelia's private investigator **Frank Mulry** noted in his July 2005 report that "subject and D. Fahey were present at the scene approx. 20 min. prior to the 911 call (witness: neighbor, 2nd floor, Mrs. Kathleen Gallagher, did not come forward)." The antagonist read this report. Hence the S1 finale note: **"Twenty minutes is a long time, Callum."**

## 4. THE CAMPUS MYSTERY — JO LAVOIE

### What happened (the final truth)
- **Victim: Josette "Jo" Lavoie,** b. April 2, 1985, Calder Falls. 20. Nursing student at Holyoke Community College; bartended Thursdays at **the Mill Race** (her grandfather's bar) and worked catering for **Pioneer Valley Catering (PVC).** Small, dark-haired, freckled, gap-toothed, laughed with her whole face, a terrible swimmer (she'd say "I float like a brick"). Wore a gold Saint Christopher medal (gave it to her half-brother Julian the week before she died — he wears it now) and small gold hoop earrings with a "J" charm on the left one.
- **The secret romance:** From June 2005 Jo was secretly seeing **Graham Ashford**, who met her at the Race. Real love on both sides; Graham was going to tell his father.
- **Saturday, August 13, 2005 — the Lethe Crossing.** Every second Saturday of August, Lethe's newly initiated senior class (the class of 2006 "Fifteen") makes **the Crossing**: dinner at **the Landing** (the society's boathouse lodge on **Gallows Island** in the Connecticut River, just below the mouth of the Calder), oaths, then at midnight they cross the river in the society's 1928 mahogany launch, **the *Hesper***, and "drink the river." PVC catered the dinner. Jo was on the staff.
  - **9:10 p.m.** Rafe Delacroix-Moore (then not yet enrolled; visiting his Corinthian contacts) delivered cocaine to the Landing dock at a senior's request. He saw "a girl in a caterer's white shirt with a disposable camera, laughing with a tall blond guy." He was high and forgot it. He doesn't know he's a witness.
  - After the ritual, ~**11:30 p.m.**, drunk, five people took the *Hesper* out for a "victory run" — against all Lethe rules (no outsiders aboard): **Graham Ashford, Owen Tierney, Archer "Archie" Whitby, Eliza Brandt** — and **Jo**, whom Graham invited aboard. Jo brought her FunSaver disposable camera.
  - **Jacket swap:** At the ritual's "shedding" the seniors had swapped jackets (a Lethe custom: you cross wearing a brother's coat). **Owen wore Graham's navy Sig Rho crew jacket, ASHFORD across the shoulders. Graham wore Owen's red Hollis Student Assembly windbreaker, TIERNEY on the back.**
  - **11:52 p.m.** Jo texted Julian: *"on the water w G. tell u everything tmrw. dont be mad"*
  - **Owen was at the helm.** Graham dared him to run the *Hesper* through the chute above **the Ladder** (the old mill dam/weir with a fish ladder, where the Calder meets the Connecticut). Owen gunned it. The launch hit the standing wave sideways. Jo, standing to photograph, was thrown; her head struck the gunwale; she went over.
  - They circled with a flashlight for eleven minutes. **Owen jumped in** — he reached her. She was conscious, clinging to the weir's iron rung, bleeding, screaming that she'd tell everyone, that they were murderers, that she'd tell the cops he was driving drunk. **Owen let go of her.** He tells himself he lost his grip. He did not. Graham, holding the flashlight from the bow, saw Owen's arm around her and then not — he has never been sure what he saw, and has spent every night since deciding.
  - Graham wanted to call 911. Owen said: call the Steward. Archie called **Dean Philip Carrow**, Lethe's Steward, at **12:20 a.m.** Carrow arrived at the Landing at **12:55** and took charge.
  - **The two hours.** Carrow ordered: everyone back to the Landing, showers, a story. At **2:30 a.m.** Archie drove Jo's Honda Civic from the PVC staff lot to the swimming pull-off above the Ladder dam and left her bag and towel on the rocks. At **3:10 a.m.** Carrow placed an "anonymous" call to Calder Falls police from the pay phone at the Route 10 Sunoco: "Someone's been swimming at the dam and didn't come out."
  - **4:15 a.m.** Carrow phoned groundskeeper **Joseph Clarke** (Henry's father): clean the *Hesper* and the boathouse "before the stewards' breakfast." Joseph hosed blood off the starboard gunwale. In the bilge he found **Jo's FunSaver camera** and her **gold "J" hoop earring**. He kept both. He told his son.
  - **Aug 14, 9:40 a.m.** Jo's body recovered below the Ladder. BAC 0.11. Death by drowning; scalp laceration and skull fracture attributed to "the rocks of the dam." ## 6. THE POWER MAP

### Hollis University
Founded 1791. ~5,400 undergrads. Campus on a bluff over the **Connecticut River**, where the **Calder River** comes in from the east over **the Ladder** (an 1840 mill dam with an iron fish ladder). Brick and Georgian granite, ivy, a white-steepled chapel with a tall square bell tower (**Founders' Chapel; the tower is "the Beacon"**), a 1902 library (**Kirkland Library**), the green (**the Commons**), freshman dorms on **Founders' Row** — Cal & Teddy in **Sutter Hall 304**; Del & Caro in **Bramwell Hall 212**, across the Commons. **Vanderhoef Boathouse** (Teddy's great-grandfather) on the river. President: **Harold Endicott Pyne**. Rival: **Danforth College**, across the river in New Hampshire. Freshman orientation Aug 27–Sept 6, 2005; **classes begin Wed Sept 7**; **fall break Fri Oct 7–Tue Oct 11** (Columbus Day Oct 10); Homecoming vs. Danforth Sat Nov 5; Thanksgiving break Nov 23–27; finals Dec 12–20.

### The town: **Calder Falls, Massachusetts**
Mill town, pop. ~9,000. Main Street (the Hollis Bookstore & photo kiosk, Calder Falls Diner, St. Anne's (French-Canadian Catholic, where Cal goes to Mass), the **Peter Pan depot** with a pay-phone bank and a TV over the ticket counter, the Calder Falls Inn), Canal Street along the old mill race, the **Calder Woolen Mill** (empty brick hulk). Townies call Hollis students "Hollies." Route 10 Sunoco. Northampton 35 minutes south.

### The townie bar: THE MILL RACE ("the Race")
- **1 Canal Street**, in the ground floor of the mill's old counting house, beside the race. A bar since Repeal (1933). Tin ceiling, a forty-foot mahogany bar salvaged from a Holyoke hotel, the race roaring under a glass panel in the floor, a Red Sox shrine (Yaz, Pudge, a 2004 World Series pennant with "FINALLY" in Augie's hand), Narragansett and PBR on tap, a pickled-egg jar no one has opened since Carter.
- **The Wall of Shame:** framed unpaid tabs of Hollis boys who became somebody: "T. Vanderhoef '80 — $212.00"; "N. Sloane '87 — $46.50"; "R. Ashford '74 — $9.25 + one window." Augie refuses Hollis money for old debts: "They can owe me. I like it."
- Jo's photo beside the register (graduation, gap-toothed grin), and a pickle jar: **FOR JO.**
- **Owner: Augustin "Augie" Lavoie**, b. Feb 1942, 63. Born upstairs. USMC, Khe Sanh 1968. Bought the Race in 1974 from his father-in-law. Widower (Rose, d. 1999). **Look:** 6'3", stooped now, white crew cut, a nose broken three times, a faded green USMC tattoo on a forearm like a ham, half-glasses on a cord, a cap from the '67 Impossible Dream team. **Voice:** gravel in a coffee can; few words; Québécois oaths ("Tabarnak," "câlice"); calls Cal "Southie" then "kid" then (S4+) "Callum." "Hollis boys break things and leave the bill." Calls Jo "ma puce."

(5) Hired Cal at Julian's request, Sunday Aug 28 — "He's not one of them, Pépère."

### LETHE — the secret senior society
- Founded **1791**, with the college. Fifteen seniors a year ("the Fifteen"). Co-ed since 1993. Alumni ~2,000 living; dense in law, State, finance, three cabinets, one Chief Justice of a state supreme court.
- **The tomb: "the Ferryhouse."** 1852, windowless dark granite cube on the river bluff at the end of River Street, Egyptian-revival lintel, an iron door with a carved oar, motto **OBLIVIO FIDES** ("forgetting is faith"). A tunnel (bricked 1931, reopened 1968) runs to the old steam plant — Henry knows.
- **The Landing:** the society's lodge and boathouse on **Gallows Island**, mid-river, below the mouth of the Calder. The *Hesper* (1928 mahogany launch).
- **Rituals:**
  - **Tap Night:** the last Thursday in April. Seniors in black walk to juniors' rooms, press a tarnished silver **obol** (cast replicas, 1852) into the junior's palm and say **"Remember nothing."** The tapped must not speak until midnight, when they walk to the Ferryhouse.
  - **The Coin:** at initiation each member writes a sealed confession (their worst secret), kept in the vault — the society's leverage and glue.
  - **River names:** every member is renamed for a shade from *Aeneid* VI (Palinurus, Misenus, Deiphobus, Dido, Marcellus…).
  - **Watches:** meetings Thursdays and Sundays, 8 p.m.
  - **The Crossing:** second Saturday of August, at the Landing. Drink the river.
  - **The River Book:** the Steward's ledger of every incident "so the society remembers what its members must forget."
- **The Steward:** an on-campus alumnus who handles crises. Since 1981: **Dean Philip Carrow**.
- **Wardens:** alumni trustees. Include Lowell Ashcombe '81, Russell Ashford '74, Theodore Vanderhoef III '80, Nate Sloane '87 (inactive — he stopped attending in 1999).
- **Class of 2006 (the Fifteen at the pilot):** includes Graham Ashford, Owen Tierney, Archie Whitby, Eliza Brandt. **Class of 2007** tapped April 27, 2006: includes **Julian Thorne** (tapped by Graham personally — his penance; Graham suspects who Julian is). **Cal's class tapped April 24, 2008.**

## 10. "IF THEY'RE HAPPY EARLY" PLAN (the norm)

Cal and Del are anchored. Assume they'll be in love and together fast. That's fine — that's the show. Rules:
1. **Aim the danger outward.** Their love is the thing everyone else tries to use: Owen (leverage), Graham (temptation of safety), Kit (temptation of partnership), Cordelia (class: "a Sloane doesn't marry a girl like that" — Cordelia sees through Del in one tea, S3, and *likes* her, which is worse), Lowell, Travis. Every antagonist move targets one of them through the other.
2. **Secrets are the wall, not doubt.** Their fights are about what they're *protecting each other from* — not about whether they love each other. Each holds a secret that would endanger the other if shared. Telling is the act of love; it's also the risk.
3. **Two arsonists escalate together.** If they're happy, give them a dare. Heists (the Corinthian's founders' lantern; the River Book; Lyme Boatworks' invoice file), break-ins (the Ferryhouse tunnel), cons run as a team (they're the best grifters on campus; the show should be *fun* when they're united). Each successful caper creates a consequence two episodes later.
4. **The secret relationship phase.** Del insists in S1 that they stay secret (she can't afford scrutiny or photos; Caro is being courted for Graham; she "belongs" on the other side of campus). Cal's pride vs. her survival. The "going public" moment is a S2 tentpole (Halloween or Homecoming).
5. **Organic fights:** jealousy (Graham/Kit), class (money, his pride vs. her cash), her lies, his recklessness (Southie fights), loyalty (Deck vs. Del; Caro vs. Cal), ambition (his career vs. her safety in public life — "You want to be a senator. I'm a felony.").
6. **Their sex life is its own storyline.** Night One is two liars telling the only truth they have. Then: secret sex (risk); jealous sex; the first time she says his name in her real accent; the first time after the Truth Night (she's Della in bed for the first time); comfort after Deck's IED; tenderness after Delphine's death; the split-season absence (charged, chaste, miserable — no one else); reunion; sex as a pact renewed. No two alike.
7. **The world vs. them** supplies the pressure: class (his scholarship conditions — a "conduct clause" Owen can trigger), her exposure, his family emergencies pulling him to Southie, the society.
8. **Split rule:** they may split at most twice in college (S6–S7 briefly, S12 seriously). Splits are about protecting each other or about a betrayal of *trust* (Owen's money), never about sex. Each split ends with a reunion that's earned and costs something.

### BENDABLE (the player shapes it)
- Who learns what first among the friends; who Del tells first after Cal (Rafe is the default).
- Whether Cal brings Del to Southie at Thanksgiving; whether Del meets the twins in S1.
- Cal's career path inside college (Assembly vs. Disputation vs. Honor Council — he can run for anything; Owen will be on the other side).
- Kit's arc temperature (rival → ally → near-love); Teddy's romance (Rafe or Julian or someone new); Caro & Henry.
- Whether Cal joins Lethe; whether Del does; whether they burn it.
- Graham's fate (prison/redemption); Archie's and Eliza's; Carrow's last act; Owen's legal outcome (prison vs. acquittal → post-grad adversary).
- Whether Hollis graduates Del.
- Rafe's sobriety path; Mikey Brennan's fate; Deck's injury severity.

### How big choices reshape seasons
- **If Cal tells Del about Ricky early** (any time before S5): the S5 reveal becomes Patrick's ("I saw") and the father letter; Del's knowledge becomes leverage Owen tries to extract from her in S11 (raising the stakes of her double-agent arc).
- **If the player pushes Del hard on her lies early:** don't advance the Truth Night more than one season. Instead she gives a better lie (the ward story moves up), and the relationship takes damage that must be repaired — she withdraws (secret relationship becomes secret *from Cal* for an episode). Persistence is rewarded with *clues*, not the confession.
- **If Cal ignores the mystery:** the mystery comes to him — Julian recruits him harder; Augie gives him Jo's phone; the antagonist's notes force him in. Never let the A-plot stall because the player chose romance or ambition.
- **If Cal is cruel to Kit/Teddy/etc.:** they remember (flags). Consequences show up as closed doors two to six episodes later, not immediate punishment.
- **If the player makes Cal honest at the Corinthian (confessing to Sloane he's not Teddy):** Sloane becomes a co-conspirator early — an earlier, warmer father-son proto-bond that makes the S5/S7 reveal more painful. Good.
- **If Cal goes to the police early** (e.g., about the dam): the institution swallows it (Kozlowski; Carrow) and the cost is real (Cal's scholarship conduct review; the Night One lie exposed early is *not* allowed — the figure's identity is unknowable to them in Y1, so an early report is "two freshmen saw someone in a blazer," which Carrow buries and Owen uses). The lie remains load-bearing because what they'd have to confess grows heavier each year.
- **Conservation of revelation:** every season delivers at least one fixed reveal and one bendable payoff of a past choice. Choices can change *who is in the room* for a reveal and *what it costs*, not whether it happens.
- **Echo discipline:** every end-card echo must pay off within 1–3 seasons.
