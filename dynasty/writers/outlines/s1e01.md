## 0. AT A GLANCE

### Fixed props and facts (all acts use these exactly)

- **The tent:** white sailcloth on the south lawn of the Big House, between "the House" (1928 limestone) and "the
  Italy" (the 1998 stucco wraparound). 600 yards west across the dark: the chapel. North: the cutting-horse barn
  (radiant-heated floors), floodlit on the party side, dark behind. The Mercy's **water tower** stands to the
  southeast, white, painted with the **R-Bar-M** brand, visible from everywhere.
- **The Ball:** $25,000 a table, for "rural children's literacy." This year's theme: *Boots & Bindings*. Centerpieces
  are hollowed-out leather-bound classics planted with succulents. An ice sculpture of the R-Bar-M brand, melting from
  the M. Silver stock troughs full of **Mercy Ranch Water** cans ($9.99 a four-pack, "made with San Isidro spring
  water"). Brisket from a Lockhart pitmaster flown in on Mercy One. A Grammy-winning Western swing band from Austin.
  A 40-foot LED wall behind the stage running **Colt's livestream** (@RanchRoyaltyColt). Valets parking Escalades in
  the hay stubble. Kilgore's horse, **Deacon**, a gray gelding with its own handler (NDA).
- **Whit's toast:** three index cards in his breast pocket. Card two holds the Cowboys joke ("I've waited for this
  longer than Dallas has waited for a Super Bowl"). He leaves **card three** on the front pew in s01 and never goes
  back for it (Tack finds it in s13). Card three reads, in Whit's hand: *"Thank you, Dad, for —"* and then nothing,
  crossed out twice.
- **The chapel:** cedar and limestone, 120 seats, a center aisle, the altar step at the **west end** where couples
  stand. Brass dedication plaque by the door: *THE CHAPEL AT THE MERCY · TO THE GLORY OF GOD AND IN THANKSGIVING FOR
  THIS LAND · AMON & LORNA RANSOME · MMIII*. Whit and Greer married here in 2019. Mercy Events LLC rents it at $38,000
  a weekend.
- **Paloma tonight:** black column dress, no jewelry except a turquoise cuff and a man's steel watch too big for her
  wrist. Her shoes (black satin, $1,100, she calls them "the invoices") lined up side by side inside the chapel's back
  door. The worn red clay **$5,000 Petroleum Club chip** over her knuckles. She **sits in the back pew and never walks
  up the aisle.**
- **Amon tonight:** black tux, silverbelly Open Road hat, left hand in his pocket (tremor — Whit notices the pocket,
  not the tremor, until s04).
- **The old room:** Amon's bedroom in the original House. Narrow iron bed, a saddle on a stand, a King James Bible
  with a rubber band around it, the hat hook, a 1970s Zenith radio, and on the nightstand **a baby monitor, the same
  model as June's**. (Never explained.) **The blue room** is at the far east end of the House. Paloma's.
- **The projection:** from a box truck on the county road (FM 2400) beyond the east fence. Three images on the water
  tower: (1) *RANSOME WATER = STOLEN WATER*; (2) *OJO VIVO · SAN ISIDRO · EL AGUA ES DE TODOS*; (3) a photo, low
  resolution, early-digital color: a smiling young man, thirties, squinting into sun, in a short-sleeved state-issue
  polo with a clipboard against his chest, and under him in white block letters: **¿Y TEO?** No surname, no dates, no
  agency name legible. Whit doesn't know the face.

## ACT ONE — "THE BACK PEW"

### s01 — INT. THE CHAPEL AT THE MERCY — NIGHT

- **POV:** Whit. Cutaway: no. **Target:** ~1,250 words.
- **Purpose:** Open on the charming coward hiding from his own coronation. Meet Paloma as a voice before a face. Set
  the comic register, then the first undertow.
- **Characters:** Whit; Paloma (voice, then seen).
- **Beats:**
  1. 9:02 p.m. Whit at the altar step of the empty chapel, tie undone, a sweating can of Mercy Ranch Water in one
     hand (spiked from a flask; Colt's product, "made with San Isidro spring water"), index cards in the other. The
     Ball roars 600 yards east: bass, a steel guitar, a whoop. He rehearses his acceptance toast to 120 empty seats
     — gracious, funny, humble in the way that's been focus-grouped by his wife. He realizes he's standing exactly
     where he stood to get married. He makes a joke about it to no one.
  2. He does the Cowboys joke from card two. It lands with nobody.
  3. From the dark back pew: *"It's better without the joke about the Cowboys."* He nearly drops the can.
  4. Paloma, in the back pew, shoes lined up inside the door "like it's a house." He knows exactly who she is — the
     whole state does: his father's wife, the card girl from Odessa, "the Pigeon." They've met at a dozen events and
     never had a real conversation. She doesn't get up. She asks him questions about the toast instead of answering
     why she's here.
  5. He puts card three — *"Thank you, Dad, for —"*, crossed out twice — face down on the front pew. (Leave it there.
     Mention it once, lightly.)
- **Satire beat:** The toast itself — Greer's bullet points ("gratitude, legacy, the next Texas, one self-deprecating
  line, no more than one") delivered to an empty sanctuary built by a man who has never attended a wedding in it
  (don't say that last part; just let Whit note that his father always sits in the back when he comes here at all).
  The $38,000 weekend rate is printed on a brochure in the pew rack.
- **Texas/money detail:** Mercy Ranch Water can; the $38,000 brochure; the $1,100 shoes.
- **Emotional turn:** Embarrassment → the strange relief of being caught by someone who also shouldn't be here.
- **Choices** (all → `s02`):
  1. **"Make it a joke. ‘I was told this room had great acoustics and no relatives.’"**
     - Intent: the dodge, flirt register. Set `e1_chapel_flirt: true`, `dodges: "+1"`. Tone: none.
     - Ledger: *"You met your father’s wife in the chapel and made it a bit."*
     - Response: She enjoys it, visibly, and then names it: *"You’re doing the thing where you’re charming at me.
       It’s very good. It’s like watching a man tap-dance in a house fire."* He laughs because she's right.
  2. **"Tell her the truth. You’re hiding."**
     - Intent: the true thing. Set `e1_chapel_honest: true`, `candor: "+1"`. Tone: `paloma: +1`.
     - Cue: *"Paloma will remember that."*
     - Ledger: *"You told Paloma you were hiding from your own party."*
     - Response: A pause. *"Okay."* She moves over six inches on the back pew, which in her is an embrace. "Me too.
       From a different party. Same tent."
  3. **"‘Mrs. Ransome.’ Button the jacket. Be the son."**
     - Intent: the move — formality as armor. Set `e1_chapel_formal: true`. Tone: `paloma: -1`.
     - Ledger: *"You called her Mrs. Ransome in an empty church."*
     - Response: *"Mr. Ransome."* Exactly as cool. Then, not unkindly: "You can button it all the way. I've seen
       the rest of the family. It doesn't help."

### s02 — INT. THE CHAPEL AT THE MERCY — CONTINUOUS

- **POV:** Whit. Cutaway: no. **Target:** ~1,250 words.
- **Purpose:** The first real conversation: two liars catching each other and liking it. Plant L0 tells and Whit's

### s05 — INT. THE HOUSE — THE OLD ROOM — NIGHT  *(Fork 1, path A)*

- **POV:** Whit. Cutaway: no. **Target:** ~950 words.
- **Purpose:** Inside the king's private room. The intimacy of a shared secret. Paloma asks for silence.
- **Characters:** Whit, Paloma, Amon.
- **Beats:**
  1. Through the dark barn aisle (warm floor through Amon's boots), across the mudroom of the original 1928 House,
     down a hall that smells like saddle soap and Pledge. Paloma knows which boards creak. Whit, who grew up here,
     doesn't.
  2. The old room: narrow iron bed, saddle on a stand, the rubber-banded Bible, the Zenith radio, the hat hook.
     **A baby monitor on the nightstand, the same model as June's.** Whit sees it and decides not to think about it.
  3. Paloma gets Amon seated, his collar open, water, his hat on the hook. Amon re-assembles himself in front of them,
     piece by piece, like a man dressing in armor: in four minutes he is nearly the lion again. He says to Whit,
     coldly and clearly: *"A man who needs to be liked will be owned by whoever likes him."* — which is Amon for
     *thank you* and *get out*.
  4. In the hall, door half-closed, Paloma and Whit very close because the hall is narrow. She asks him: nobody. Not
